## Chapter 13

I don't flinch, and I don't beg.

Instead, I open my fingers slowly and let the heavy **coiled VHF handset** settle onto the mahogany blotter beside Victor Garroway's open laptop. The brass clip clicks softly against the wood. Behind me, **Margot Kiefer-Hall** shifts her weight on the pine floorboards, her breathing shallow and tight in the warm kerosene haze, the compact `.380` muzzle still grinding into the hollow beneath my occipital bone.

"Hands where I can see them, Tessa," she says, her voice stripped of the clipped Back Bay polish she wore in the hallway. "Step away from the desk. Now."

"Look at the screen, Margot." I keep my palms flat on the mahogany edge, fingers spread wide so she can see my bruised, salt-chapped knuckles are empty. "Before you pull that trigger, look at what your partner just did while you were tending his stove."

"I'm not looking at your staging."

"It's his laptop, his encrypted VPN, and your gold token in the USB slot." I nod slightly toward the green banking window glowing against the dark pane. "Your leather card case is sitting right beside the trackpad, half-open where he tossed it. Check the timestamp. `9:15 PM`."

For two heartbeats, the only sound in the alcove is the soft tick of the kerosene heater and sleet rattling against the shuttered glass. Then Margot takes a half-step to her left, keeping the `.380` lined up on the base of my neck while her eyes drop past my shoulder to the glowing green banner.

Even without turning my head, I feel Margot go dead still behind my shoulder.

**`TRANSFER AMOUNT: $6,400,000.00 (REMAINING ESCROW RESERVE)`**
**`ORIGIN: STATE STREET ESCROW #884-19B (K-H FIDUCIARY)`**
**`BENEFICIARY: HARBOR REACH HOLDINGS LTD. — GRAND CAYMAN (SOLE SIGNATORY: V. GARROWAY)`**

"He's draining `#884-19B`," I say quietly, watching her reflection stiffen in the black windowpane. "Every last cent of the Boston escrow reserve. He lifted your hardware token out of your coat pocket while you were arguing over 1998 trust papers, and he wired the whole reserve to a sole-signature Cayman shell."

"That's a contingency transfer," Margot whispers, though the certainty has drained out of her voice. "We discussed offshore liquidity—"

"Did you discuss **`Harbor Reach Holdings`** with your name left off the signatory line?" I lift my left hand slowly, palm open, and point toward the small thermal printer beside the router. "I'm going to reach into my coat pocket with two fingers. Just paper. No steel."

She doesn't stop me, though the `.380` tracks my elbow as I slide the folded **thermal wire receipt** out of my wet wool pocket and lay it flat beside her leather card case. Margot's free hand darts down, snatches the narrow curl of paper, and tilts it toward the green glow of the screen.

Her eyes scan the authorization code, the `9:15 PM` timestamp, and the Grand Cayman routing string. Her thumb creases the thermal slip hard enough to tear the edge.

"He suspects you," I tell her, keeping my voice low so it doesn't carry down the hall toward the front vestibule. "He knows you didn't skip half a million in federal bail just to help him burn crates in the dark. He's emptying your vault, leaving your fingerprints on the ash tubs by the stove, and letting the Marshals pick up whatever's left of you at dawn."

***

Margot lowers the thermal slip onto the blotter, her fingers trembling once before she forces them still. The `.380` muzzle drops away from my skull, sliding back a pace as she steps clear of the doorway and keeps the barrel leveled squarely at the center of my chest.

"Turn around," she orders. "Slowly."

I turn, keeping my hands clear of my coat, and rest my hip against the edge of the mahogany desk. Up close, the stove heat hasn't erased the gray exhaustion beneath her eyes. Her tailored wool coat smells of expensive bergamot and woodsmoke, and her right hand holds the compact steel frame steady enough to mean business. Rainwater drips from the hem of my coat onto the pine floorboards, pooling between us.

"You were in the archival file room," she says, her gaze dropping to the faded navy duffel resting against my wet boots. "I heard the lock turn twenty minutes ago. You have my grandfather's 1998 charter-trust instruments."

"I do."

"Put them on the desk."

"No."

Margot's thumb shifts against the safety catch, a crisp metallic click in the quiet alcove. "Don't test me, Tessa. I'm already facing twenty years on the *Caretaker's Ledger* indictment. One more body in a flooded basement doesn't change my sentencing guidelines."

"If you shoot me, Victor hears the shot from the pier," I answer evenly. "He walks back in here, steps over me, takes the 1998 charter instruments out of my duffel, and feeds them into his cherry-red drum stove right in front of you. Then you have no cooperation package for Nora Halvorsen, no `$6,400,000` in Boston, and no ride off this island at the `00:30` ebb."

Margot's jaw tightens. She glances once toward the dark hallway, listening to the empty house, then back at the green Cayman transfer still pulsing on Garroway's screen.

"What do you want?" she asks.

"I want off this island alive, and I want Garroway in federal custody before he dumps **Crate `#06`** into the Block Island trench." I keep my eyes locked on hers. "The charter instruments stay zipped inside my duffel. That's the only insurance I have that you don't put a round in my chest and walk back to the stove."

"And what's my insurance that you don't burn them yourself—or hand Halvorsen the rest of that file room before I even reach a proffer table?"

She doesn't know I already have her signed **2003–2025 survivor annuity ledger** buried beneath the 1998 charter instruments in the oilskin liner. And I'm not about to tell her.

"We hold a truce until dawn," I say. "You don't shoot me, and you stall Garroway if he comes looking for the file-room boxes. In return, I keep your 1998 charter instruments dry inside my duffel, and I don't let them near his stove. When the Coast Guard or Halvorsen's team steps onto this island, we settle up on dry land."

Margot studies my face for three long seconds, weighing the math the way every State Street lawyer weighs a bad deal—calculating which betrayal hurts less before morning. Her eyes drop once to the `.380` in her hand, then back to the thermal receipt crumpled against her palm. Neither of us trusts the other to breathe without watching the hands.

"Stay out of the main house," she whispers, lowering the `.380` an inch toward her coat pocket without taking her finger off the guard. "If Victor finds you inside, I won't take a bullet for you."

"I wouldn't ask you to."

While her eyes flick back to the Cayman wire on the laptop screen, I reach down, scoop the coiled VHF handset cleanly off the blotter, and slide it back into my deep wool pocket beside the blue ballpoint pen, the thermal receipt, and my father's brass master key. Margot sees the movement too late, her lips thinning, then lets it go—because she needs the Coast Guard on this island almost as badly as I do.

***

Outside the front portico, frozen gravel crunches under two pairs of heavy boots.

A sharp metallic *tick-thud* strikes the bottom granite step—Conrad Vale's steel toe-tap—followed immediately by Victor Garroway's measured stride coming up from the south path. The wind rattles the porch storm door against its brass chain.

Margot's head snaps toward the front vestibule. "Window," she hisses, already backing out of the alcove toward the corridor. "Now. Before he clears the porch."

She pivots down the hall toward the Chief Surgeon's Office, her heels clicking briskly on the pine boards as she moves to meet Garroway by the stove and keep him out of the front rooms.

I grab my navy duffel by the canvas strap, sling it tight across my chest, and reach for the narrow wooden latch beside the swollen lower storm sash. My bruised fingers sting as I twist the brass thumb-turn free. The painted pine frame sticks hard in its track—swollen from forty years of salt fog—so I slide the crowbar from my duffel webbing, wedge its flat tip under the bottom rail, and pry upward with both shoulders.

With a sharp crack of breaking ice and old putty, the sash slides open, admitting a freezing blast of sleet and salt spray off the side yard. The kerosene space heater sputters behind me as the cold draft hits the alcove.

In the front vestibule, the heavy exterior oak door groans on its iron hinges.

"Margot?" Garroway's calm voice carries cleanly down the corridor. "Bring the kerosene can. The stove's—"

I roll over the sill feet-first, ducking my head as the duffel clears the frame, and drop onto the frozen gravel along the granite foundation. The impact jars my bruised right knee and sends a fresh jolt up my left hip. Without standing upright, I reach back up, pull the swollen sash down until the glass meets the sill, and press myself flat into the dark shadow beneath the cellar bulkhead just as the alcove light blinks out.

Sleet needles my neck and hands as I tuck the duffel tight under my ribs. Yellow flashlight beams sweep across the wet granite porch steps and cut through the driving sleet toward the Coal Pier. My freezing palm scrapes against the rough mortar as I hold my breath, waiting for Vale's boot to round the corner of the foundation.

Instead, the heavy oak portico door swings wide again, and Garroway steps out onto the granite porch threshold, his breath pluming white against the dark lintel as he looks out over the black, rain-lashed courtyard toward the marsh. A faint cherry ember glows in the bowl of his briar pipe, sending a thin ribbon of pipe smoke straight into the wind. My wet wool coat clings to my ribs like cold iron as I wait in the dark.

He doesn't sweep his light into the foundation shadow, and he doesn't step off the granite porch. He doesn't even raise his voice above a level, conversational carry that rides cleanly over the wind and the booming flood tide.

"Halvorsen's keeping Nina in a nice place, Tessa," Victor Garroway calls into the dark. "I know which one."
