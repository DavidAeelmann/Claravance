# 01 — Anti-AI Prose Style Guide (*The Quarantine Manifest*)

## 1. Point of View & Tense Contract
- **Chapters 1–23 (Tessa Calder — 100% Locked POV):** Strict **first-person present tense (`I`)**. Immediate, forensic, hyper-observant, dry under pressure.
- **No Third-Person Flashback Chapters:** Never switch into third-person past-tense flashbacks. All 1998 (*M/V Northern Cross* / Barrow Island) and 2014 (Thomas Calder's murder) revelations must be uncovered organically by Tessa through physical documents, manifest logs, specimens, and live confrontations.

## 2. Hard-Banned Lexicon (Zero-Tolerance Audit List)
Any occurrence in a draft triggers automatic Critic rejection and rewrite:
- **Abstract LLM Nouns/Metaphors:** `testament`, `tapestry`, `mosaic`, `symphony`, `cacophony`, `kaleidoscope`, `beacon`, `labyrinth`, `abyss`, `chasm`, `maelstrom`, `facade`, `orchestration`, `enigma`, `paradox`.
- **Sensory & Thriller Clichés:** `ozone`, `palpable`, `shiver down`, `chill down`, `ice in [my/her] veins`, `blood ran cold`, `breath hitched`, `let out a breath`, `knuckles turned white`, `silence stretched`, `eyes narrowed`, `throat tightened`, `stomach dropped`, `wave of dread`, `wave of relief`, `wave of panic`, `flicker of`, `chuckle`, `smirk`, `steely gaze`.
- **Syntactic Crutches:** `"It wasn't [X]—it was [Y]"` / `"It wasn't [X]; it was [Y]"`, `"Little did [I/she] know"`, `"In that moment"`, `"Nothing would ever be the same"`, `"Before I knew it"`, `"A reminder that"`.

## 3. Structural & Craft Rules
1. **Asymmetric Sentence Cadence:** Never write three medium sentences (12–16 words) consecutively. Alternate short, blunt fragments (2–6 words) with crisp observational clauses (18–26 words).
2. **Participial Opener Cap:** Maximum 1 sentence starting with an `-ing` verb phrase per 500 words (ban *"Walking into the vault, I saw..."* → *"I pushed the tin-clad door open. Formalin stung my nostrils."*).
3. **No Summary or Moralizing Scene Endings:** Never close a scene or chapter with Tessa summarizing what she learned or how she feels. **Cut two beats early** on a concrete object, physical contradiction, or interrupted motion.
4. **Cornered Thriller Protagonist (Interior Stakes > Stoic Noir):** Put the reader inside Tessa's personal desperation, exhaustion, and claustrophobia—what it costs her to survive an unheated quarantine island while her mother's remains and Nina's safety hang in the balance.
5. **The "No Tape Measure" Rule (Cap Numerical Measurements):** Eliminate surveyor geometry (inches, feet, yards, degrees, pounds, fractions, exact seconds). Replace measurements with visceral impression (*narrow, low, freezing, heavy*). Reserve exact numbers **strictly** for financial math, temperatures (`4°C`), and forensic timestamps/dates. Maximum 4 physical unit numbers per chapter.
6. **Cut Redundant "Negative Lists":** Never describe rooms by listing multiple absent items. Use the absent-object device at most **once per chapter**, only when the absence is a forensic clue.
7. **Purposeful Micro-Actions Only:** Do not narrate granular body/object mechanics unless Tessa is deliberately setting a tamper-trap—and if she is, give half a sentence of interior thought so the reader knows why.
8. **Plausible Clue Placement:** Meticulous antagonists never leave smoking guns in plain sight by accident. Either bury clues inside logical blind spots (sealed autoclave flues, shredder bags, false bulkheads) or explicitly register Tessa's paranoia that the clue was planted as bait or left mid-purge.
9. **Subtext-First Dialogue:** Characters lie by deflecting to logistics, maritime law, or underwriting procedure. Use `said` or physical action beats for 90%+ of attributions; ban adverbs on dialogue tags (*"he said coldly"*).
10. **Non-Visual Sensory Grounding (No 3-Item Smell Lists):** Anchor scenes with one unexpected, sharp sensory detail (medical formalin, wet cedar shavings, Latakia tobacco, 60-cycle transformer hum)—never a formulaic three-item list (`smelling of X, Y, and Z`).
11. **Zero "Chapter-Reset" Inventory/Backstory Recaps:** Introduce Tessa's scars, `$41,000` balance, Civic, and `WARD C` key once when active. Never start or pad chapters with pocket pat-downs or backstory recitals.
12. **Somatic Cliché & Pivot Bans:** Never use `"Wait."` / `"—Wait."` monologue pivots or `"Not X. Just Y."` contrastive fragments. Cap `pulse`, `stomach`, `heartbeats`, `blink`, `jaw tightens`, and `cold sweat` to at most 1–2 organic uses across the entire book.
13. **Separate Observation from Deduction:** Plant physical evidence and let the reader connect the dots a beat before Tessa explains it—no immediate three-sentence forensic lectures upon finding a clue. Vary scene-to-scene investigation and escape mechanics.

## 4. Scene-Expansion Pacing Rule
- Each ~1,750–2,200-word chapter MUST be drafted across **3 discrete ~600–750-word scene beats** (Entry/Friction → Escalation/Subtext → Physical Discovery/Early Cut). Never compress or summarize transitions.
