## PART A — POTENTIAL PLOT HOLES & MOTIVATIONAL / CONTINUITY ISSUES

### OQ-01: Why Did Lydia Order Cole Halstead to Plant Her Packed Weekender Bag—Bearing an `OCT 17` Bar Harbor–Yarmouth Ferry Tag—Inside Graham's Coat Closet? (`[HIGH]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 1 (lines 119–143)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 2 (lines 31–35), Chapter 7 (line 35), Chapter 14 (lines 105–107), Chapter 17 (lines 53–54).
- **The Issue:**
  - In Chapter 1, on Monday night (Nov 2), Tessa opens the front entry coat closet and discovers Lydia's packed olive-canvas weekender (`L.K.`) hidden behind Graham's wool overcoats, carrying a perforated blue-ink ferry luggage tag stamped **`BAR HARBOR–YARMOUTH — SLIP 4 / OCT 17`**—three days *after* Lydia supposedly drowned on **October 14**.
  - In Chapter 14 (line 107), Graham says the bag wasn't there Sunday night and that Cole Halstead stood alone in the vestibule for five minutes on Monday morning (Nov 2).
  - In Chapter 17 (lines 53–54), when Tessa says `"Right. That's why Cole had to hide your weekender behind Graham's coats"`, Lydia snaps back: **`"I told him to drop it there Monday morning. Same way I had to babysit him November first when we staged the sunroom floor..."`**
- **Why This Is a Motivational Paradox:**
  - Lydia and Cole's entire scheme is to frame Graham for **murdering Lydia in the sunroom on the night of October 14** and dumping her body via the boathouse at 2:14 AM on October 15!
  - Why would Lydia intentionally order Cole to plant her packed travel bag with an **October 17 Nova Scotia ferry tag** inside Graham's coat closet, when an `OCT 17` ferry tag indicates Lydia was *alive and boarding a ferry to Canada three days after her alleged murder*—which is the very clue that tips Tessa off on Night 1 that Lydia didn't drown on October 14?!
- **Recommended Authorial Resolution (1-Sentence Fix in Ch. 17, line 54):**
  - Instead of Lydia saying she *wanted* the `OCT 17` ferry tag found in Graham's closet, have Lydia say in Ch. 17, line 54 that she brought the weekender back from Nova Scotia (or told Cole to plant her bag so it looked like Graham kept her runaway gear after killing her on Oct 17, OR better yet: **Lydia told Cole to stash her bag out of sight when he picked her up, and Cole stupidly shoved it into the front closet without ripping the `OCT 17` Yarmouth ferry tag off the handle**—matching her very next sentence: `"the idiot dropped his pilfered B-4 key behind the baseboard"`!). Framing the unremoved `OCT 17` ferry tag as Cole's blunder (just like dropping the `B-4` key) makes Lydia's anger at Cole's sloppiness in Ch. 17, lines 54–55 100% logical and eliminates the motivational paradox!

### OQ-02: How Can the Front Entry Door Deadbolt Simultaneously Be a Remote Smart-Lock (Ch. 1), Thrown Shut from Inside Without a Key by Cole (Ch. 19), and a "Double-Keyed Lock" Requiring Tessa's Brass Key from the Inside (Ch. 22)? (`[HIGH]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 1 (lines 23, 95)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 19 (lines 15–17, 39), Chapter 22 (lines 19–21).
- **The Issue:**
  - In Ch. 1, line 23: `"Before I can knock, the smart-lock deadbolt snaps open on its own."`
  - In Ch. 19, line 39: `"Heavy boots hit the front slate overhead, followed by the rattle of the front deadbolt throwing shut from inside. Cole is back from the causeway."` (At this point Cole does *not* have Graham's key ring, because in line 51 Lydia tells him: `"Grab Graham's key ring off the study desk"`).
  - In Ch. 22, lines 19–21: `"I dig the brass front-door key out of my jeans with my free hand and toss it across the vestibule to Graham. 'Unlock the deadbolt. Now.' Graham catches it against his chest, stares at me for half a beat, then turns and works the double-keyed lock beside the sidelight."`
- **Recommended Authorial Resolution:**
  - Either (a) the front door deadbolt has a standard interior thumb-turn (so in Ch. 22, line 19–21 Tessa simply orders Graham `"Unlock the deadbolt. Now"` and he turns the interior deadbolt latch without needing Tessa to toss him a key), OR (b) if the author wants to preserve the dramatic beat of Tessa tossing Graham the brass key in Ch. 22, specify in Ch. 19, line 39 that Cole used a spare house key on his sheriff's ring to lock the double-keyed interior cylinder.

### OQ-03: Why Do Tessa and Graham Wait Seven Hours on Thursday (6:45 AM – 3:30 PM) Before Taking Two Minutes to Decrypt the Master Thumb Drive or Move the Muddy Boots Out of the Foyer? (`[HIGH]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 14 (lines 109–117)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 15 (lines 3–39).
- **The Issue:**
  - At 6:30 AM Thursday (Ch. 14), Tessa and Graham both know that Lydia is alive, Cole Halstead is her accomplice, someone locked Tessa in the cellar at 2:34 AM, and someone popped Tessa's Civic trunk and planted Lydia's muddy duck boots, a bleach rag, and a pried boathouse cleat to frame both of them for murder.
  - Yet when Tessa demands the 32-character passphrase for the thumb drive at 6:45 AM, Graham refuses until she finishes seven hours of upstairs closet photography (`"Not until you finish the upstairs inventory photos for the Arlington probate packet... Shoot the rooms; I'll mount the drive this afternoon"`), and at 3:30 PM (Ch. 15, lines 3–7) the muddy duck boots are *still* sitting in a brackish puddle on the foyer floor and Tessa's Civic trunk is still open in the mist!
- **Recommended Authorial Resolution:**
  - Add a concrete technical reason in Ch. 14, line 117 / Ch. 15, line 15 why the encrypted thumb drive/partition cannot be mounted until 3:30 PM—for example, the intruder's three failed passphrase attempts at 4:45 PM Wednesday (established in Ch. 12, line 19!) triggered a **24-hour / multi-hour brute-force security time-lock or RAID parity rebuild** that does not clear until 3:30 PM Thursday! That uses an existing clue from Chapter 12 to make the 3:30 PM decryption window 100% unavoidable.

## PART B — MINOR UNEXPLAINED CLUES & PROCEDURAL QUESTIONS

### OQ-04: Who Painted Lydia's Upstairs Studio Sash Window Shut, and Why? (`[UNRESOLVED]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 2 (lines 59–65)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 3 (line 17), Chapter 9 (line 87).
- **The Issue:**
  - In Ch. 2 (lines 59–65), Tessa checks the seaward wooden sash window in Lydia's studio because the broker's intake sheet noted Lydia always kept it cracked even in October storms to prevent turpentine migraines. Tessa finds the seam bridged solid with fresh, cured white semi-gloss paint without pry marks (`"Maybe maintenance painted it shut months ago. Or maybe somebody sealed it after Lydia vanished—to kill a draft, or to bury tool marks on the sill"`).
  - In Ch. 9 (line 87), Tessa lists `"painting the studio window shut"` alongside bleaching the sunroom floor and planting the `OCT 17` weekender as part of the homicide staging.
  - However, neither Graham, Lydia, nor Cole ever explains *why* the studio window was painted shut or who painted it. (Did Lydia and Cole paint it shut so Graham or investigators would think someone sealed the room or so Lydia wouldn't appear to have left a window open during the gale? Or did Graham paint it shut?).
- **Assessment:** Can either remain as minor background staging or be given a five-word explanation in Ch. 14 or Ch. 15 (e.g., on the storm audio or in Graham's explanation).

### OQ-05: Who Searched Tessa's Duffel and Warmed Her Laptop Battery on Tuesday Afternoon while She Was in Town? (`[UNRESOLVED / INTENTIONAL AMBIGUITY]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 6 (lines 7, 33–43, 51–58)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 7 (line 17), Chapter 14 (line 65–67), Chapter 15 (line 21).
- **The Issue:**
  - On Tuesday afternoon (Nov 3), while Tessa is in Salt Point harbor (Ch. 5), someone enters her unlocked guest room, breaks the telltale lining thread on her duffel zipper, leaves the $5,000 cash untouched, and boots up her laptop (stopped by her 12-character passphrase). Outside, fresh deep-lugged tire tracks loop through the gravel turnaround, and inside, Graham is cooking leek soup and claims Cole Halstead only checked the gate keypad and `"didn't even come inside."`
  - Later, on Wednesday afternoon (likely during the 4:30–5:15 PM window when Tessa is at Maren's shed and Lydia tries the basement server at 4:45 PM), someone opens the outer side pouch of Tessa's duffel and steals her **spare Civic key fob** (discovered in Ch. 15, line 21).
- **Analysis:**
  - Either (1) **Graham** searched Tessa's duffel on Tuesday afternoon while she was in town (consistent with his Oct 21 vetting memo `"Watch what Oct 15 tampering she flags first"`!) and **Lydia** later stole the spare Civic key fob on Wednesday afternoon at 4:45 PM, OR (2) **Lydia** (who had just returned from St. Andrews, NB and triggered the Basement Zone 4 motion alert during Tuesday's dinner blackout at the end of Ch. 6!) crept up via the dumbwaiter shaft while Graham was occupied and searched Tessa's duffel on Tuesday afternoon.
- **Assessment:** Both possibilities are completely consistent with the text.

### OQ-06: Why Did Graham Keep the Three Sliced Sketchbook Pages Covered in His Penciled `L.K.` Forgery Practice Runs Inside His `OCT 14 — MASTER BACKUP` Envelope? (`[MEDIUM]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 2 (line 79)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 13 (lines 31–37), Chapter 14 (lines 45–55), Chapter 20 (lines 33–63), Chapter 22 (line 79).
- **The Issue:**
  - In Ch. 13 (line 35), Tessa opens Graham's red-taped `OCT 14 — MASTER BACKUP` envelope and finds his notarized October 10 trust ratification packet ($2.4M) alongside `"the three blank pages cut out of her studio sketchbook, covered in dozens of penciled practice runs of L.K., complete with light-table registration ticks along the margins."`
  - Why would Graham slice three blank pages out of Lydia's linen sketchbook just to use as practice scratch paper on October 10, and why would he preserve those incriminating practice sheets inside his master backup envelope instead of destroying them on October 10?
- **Recommended Authorial Resolution:**
  - Note in Ch. 13, line 35 that the three pages sliced from the back of Lydia's sketchbook carried **Lydia's authentic signed preliminary sketches/initials** (which Graham sliced out to use as his **light-table master tracing templates**, adding his pencil registration ticks and practice runs in the margins) and locked inside his steel archive cabinet so no one would find them in his study! That explains *both* why he had to slice pages out of her sketchbook *and* why he locked them in the safe instead of leaving them out.

### OQ-07: How Did Tessa Obtain Lydia's Full U.S. Passport Number from a Public Coast Guard Missing-Person Bulletin? (`[MEDIUM]`)
- **Originating Chapter:** Chapter 5 (line 59)
- **Subsequent Chapters:** Chapter 10 (lines 105–117).
- **The Issue:**
  - In Ch. 5, line 59, Tessa sits in her car in Salt Point harbor, pulls up `"the public Coast Guard missing-person bulletin for Lydia Kiefer"`, copies her passport number off the notice, and texts it to Samira Patel. In real life, public Coast Guard bulletins do not publish a missing citizen's full 9-digit passport number (`#••••••3907`).
- **Recommended Authorial Resolution:**
  - Have Tessa pull Lydia's passport number from the estate intake file / travel folder in the house (or let Samira query the *Atlantic Marine Underwriters* port-clearance watch using Lydia's full name, DOB, and vessel registration from the Coast Guard bulletin).

- **Details:**
  - At Becky's Diner on Monday, December 7, 2026, Margot Kiefer-Hall (*Kiefer-Hall Fiduciary*, Boston) offers Tessa $45,000 for a 10-day winter inventory of a snowbound mountain lodge in northern Vermont (legally empty since October, caretaker walked off Tuesday, smoke curling from the chimney).
  - On the back of the lodge photograph is a cropped black-and-white fax printout of a lined paper guest ledger dated **two days ago (Saturday, December 5, 2026)** bearing three words in the margin beside a room number in the cramped, back-slanted cursive of Tessa's younger sister **Nina Calder** (who vanished in Vermont six years ago): **`*Don't trust him.*`**
  - Tessa's final thought of Book 1 explicitly frames the intentional ambiguity: `"Across the Formica, Margot watches me with her gloved hands folded—and I don't know which is worse: that she has no idea whose handwriting she just put in front of me, or that she knew before she ever walked through the door."`
- **Protection Instruction:** **DO NOT ALTER OR RESOLVE IN BOOK 1.** This is the primary narrative bridge to Book 2 (*The Caretaker's Ledger*).
