## 4. Logic and Plausibility
- **[MEDIUM] Convenience of the Wedged Oyster Knife & Villain Monologue Tropes (Ch. 17, lines 35, 43–65):**
  - *Observation:* Finding a rusted oyster knife wedged blade-out under the bait-table rail inches above Tessa's zip-tied wrists—followed by Lydia delivering a full explanation of her scheme right over a glowing blue microphone LED—leans on classic thriller genre conventions.
  - *Mitigating Factors in the Text:*
    1. An old oyster knife wedged into a bait-table rail seam inside a coastal Maine boathouse stacked with traps is environmentally natural.
    2. Tessa has to *deliberately provoke* Lydia's ego (accusing Lydia of being an amateur who let a county deputy run her plan and claiming Boston will freeze her Cayman money for five years) to make Lydia step over to the bait table and brag.
    3. Sawing through thick marine nylon takes prolonged effort and badly lacerates Tessa's left wrist, with real physical consequences across Chapters 18–23 (she cannot grip the iron ladder rungs with her left hand in Ch. 19 or 21 and requires stitches in Ch. 22).
  - *High-Priority Continuity Question Raised by Lydia's Monologue (Line 54):* As flagged in Chapter 1, Lydia says in line 54: `"I told him to drop it [the weekender bag] there Monday morning."` If Lydia intentionally ordered Cole to plant her packed weekender bag in Graham's coat closet on Monday morning, **why did that bag have an `OCT 17` Bar Harbor–Yarmouth ferry tag attached to the handle** when Lydia's disappearance was staged as an **October 14** drowning? (See Cross-Chapter Investigation & `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`: if Lydia simply says in line 54 `"I told him to stow my bag out of sight Monday morning—and the idiot left the October seventeenth Yarmouth ferry tag hanging right on the handle"`, it turns a plot paradox into another concrete example of Cole's sloppiness alongside dropping the `B-4` key!).
