## 4. Logic and Plausibility
- **[MEDIUM] Why Would the Broker's Intake Sheet Mention Lydia's Turpentine Migraines?**
  - *Passage (Ch. 2, line 59):* `"I push the drawer shut and remember a throwaway line from the broker's intake sheet: Lydia hated central air and kept her studio window cracked even in October storms so the turpentine wouldn't set off migraines."`
  - *Explanation:* Why would an estate-packing broker's intake sheet include a personal medical/habit detail that the deceased homeowner's wife hated central air and kept her studio window cracked during October storms to avoid turpentine migraines? While convenient for alerting Tessa that the painted-shut window is anomalous, it reads slightly like an authorial clue-plant unless the intake sheet had a "Property quirks / HVAC / ventilation notes" section warning the packer not to seal or force studio windows.
- **[UNRESOLVED] What Purpose Did Painting the Studio Window Shut Serve?**
  - *Passage (Ch. 2, lines 61–65):* `"Fresh white semi-gloss paint bridges the seam between sash and frame, cured solid without a single pry mark. Maybe maintenance painted it shut months ago. Or maybe somebody sealed it after Lydia vanished—to kill a draft, or to bury tool marks on the sill."`
  - *Cross-Reference:* In Ch. 9 (line 87), Tessa summarizes: `"bleaching the sunroom floor, painting the studio window shut, shoving an October seventeenth ferry bag behind Graham's coats"`. However, neither Graham, Lydia, nor Cole ever explains *why* the studio window was painted shut (or who painted it shut). Unlike the bleached sunroom floor, the sliced sketchbook pages, the B-4 key, the P-trap paste, and the Montblanc, the painted-shut studio window never receives an explicit explanation in Ch. 14–23.
