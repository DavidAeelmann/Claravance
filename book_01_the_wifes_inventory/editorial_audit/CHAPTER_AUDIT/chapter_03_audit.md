- **Main Events:**
  1. Graham opens the studio door late Monday night, checks the desk blotter and closed sketchbook, and questions why Tessa is awake. Tessa claims she was counting flat-file drawers to estimate box needs.
  2. When Graham claims he left the room exactly the way Lydia had it for the lawyers, Tessa slips and asks, `"Even the brushes?"` before covering. Passing him in the doorway, she smells cedar shavings and cold rain on him. As she descends the stairs, Graham locks the studio door with a key. Tessa wedges her guest room desk chair under her doorknob before sleeping.
  3. At 7:30 AM Tuesday, at low tide, Graham serves French roast coffee and questions Tessa about how Harbor Mutual investigated marine losses and estate fights.
  4. Graham reveals his financial pressure: Lydia's estranged brother in Zurich is pressuring the Boston trustees at **Bramwell & Shaw** to freeze their joint accounts until a formal death certificate clears. Lydia's trust holds **$5.4 million** (Graham is a joint signatory). A freeze would stall Kiefer Soundworks' pending patent buyout in escrow with an Arlington defense prime, causing Graham to miss R&D payroll next Wednesday and lose his lab lease by Thanksgiving.
  5. Graham explains he hired Tessa via a blind broker because local Camden estate crews gossip to **Deputy Halstead**, who already has three reporters poking around the causeway.
  6. Graham gives his version of October 14: Lydia took the fiberglass skiff out around 10:30 PM to check the mooring line before the wind shifted while Graham was in the basement darkroom running an acoustic sweep with noise-canceling headphones on; when he came up at midnight, the dock light was off and her rain slicker was gone; the Coast Guard found the boat at dawn drifting half a mile past the breakwater with oars still shipped, and Deputy Halstead logged it before noon.
  7. Tessa steps out onto the seaward deck and inspects the steep, unrailed granite cliff steps and the dark timber boathouse below (secured by a brass padlock and a faded county paper seal torn cleanly in two).
  8. Down on the mudflat below the boathouse, an older woman in a yellow oilskin bib with a thick silver braid (**Maren Lindqvist**) loads wire traps onto a skiff, looks up at Tessa, checks Graham's silhouette behind the dining glass, presses her palm flat to her chest, holds **two fingers** stiffly out beside her hip, and jerks her chin toward the locked boathouse before rowing into the fog.
  9. Graham comes out onto the deck with a legal pad and clipboard, warns Tessa not to waste time talking to Maren Lindqvist (claiming she's fought them over the cove boundary line for six years), and directs Tessa to start logging the sunroom rugs.
- **Purpose Within the Overall Story:** Escalates the cat-and-mouse tension between Tessa and Graham; reveals the $5.4M Bramwell & Shaw trust / Arlington defense buyout financial motive; establishes Graham's official October 14 timeline (10:30 PM departure, midnight discovery, basement headphones alibi); introduces Deputy Halstead by name and Maren Lindqvist in person; and plants two critical visual clues from the deck (the torn county paper seal on the boathouse doors and Maren's two-finger gesture toward the boathouse).

## 3. Continuity Verification
- **[CONSISTENT] Graham's Financial Cover Story vs. Actual Forgery Revelation (Ch. 3, lines 55–75 vs. Ch. 10, lines 61–69 & Ch. 14, lines 44–63):**
  - In Ch. 3, Graham gives a partial truth (the $5.4M trust at Bramwell & Shaw, the Arlington defense buyout, the threat of a freeze) while hiding the $2.4M unauthorized draws and the October 15 Notice of Default. He even drops a tell in line 75: `"If the filing clears on time, the buyout closes—and I have the money to clear every company obligation before an audit ever touches it."` This aligns seamlessly with Ch. 10 and Ch. 14.
- **[CONSISTENT] Graham's October 14 Alibi Revision (Ch. 3, line 85 vs. Ch. 14, lines 60–63):**
  - In Ch. 3, Graham claims he came upstairs from the basement darkroom at **midnight** and found the dock light off and her rain slicker gone. In Ch. 14 (lines 60–63), Tessa confronts him (`"You told the Coast Guard you came upstairs at midnight"`) and he admits he lied to the Coast Guard because he didn't want to admit staying in the cellar until **3:00 AM** when the grid dropped.
- **[UNRESOLVED] Why Did Graham Smell of Cedar Shavings and Cold Rain in Ch. 3 (Line 25)?**
  - *Passage (Ch. 3, line 25):* `"He smells faintly of cedar shavings and cold rain, odd for a man who hasn't been outside since I arrived."`
  - *Cross-Reference:* In Ch. 7 (line 27), when Tessa enters the master bedroom, she observes: `"Unlike Lydia's studio down the hall, it smells of cedar and cold rain instead of turpentine."`
  - *Explanation:* Because Graham went up to the master suite in Ch. 1 (line 99) and his own bedroom smells of cedar and cold rain (perhaps from cedar closet blocks and damp sliders/air), the scent actually matches the master bedroom! However, because Tessa calls it `"odd for a man who hasn't been outside since I arrived"` in Ch. 3 and never explicitly connects it to the identical phrase in Ch. 7 (`"smells of cedar and cold rain"`), it functions either as a subtle red herring (making the reader suspect Graham went outside Monday night) or a minor linguistic repetition between Ch. 3 and Ch. 7.
