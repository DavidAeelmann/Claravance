  8. Hanging her wet waxed jacket in the front entry/mudroom, Tessa notices the right half of the rubber boot tray has been wiped clean (missing a smaller pair of boots) and discovers a tightly packed olive-canvas weekender bag monogrammed `L.K.` hidden behind Graham's wool overcoats in the coat closet.
  9. The weekender bears an unlocked tarnished padlock and a perforated ferry luggage tag stamped `BAR HARBOR–YARMOUTH — SLIP 4` and dated `OCT 17`—three days after Lydia supposedly drowned. Footsteps sound upstairs approaching the stairs.
- **Purpose Within the Overall Story:** Establishes protagonist Tessa Calder's voice, investigative background (blacklisted Harbor Mutual fraud adjuster), financial desperation ($18,412 judgment threat), the isolated gothic-modern setting of Salt Point, the contractual trap ($50,000 NDA), Graham's fast-tracked Friday deadline, and the inciting physical anomaly (Lydia's packed weekender with an `OCT 17` ferry tag three days post-drowning).

## 3. Continuity Verification
- **[CRITICAL] Timeline / Elapsed-Days Arithmetic Contradiction (`Oct 14` vs. `Nov 2` = 19 Days vs. Day-of-Week Calendar):**
  - *Passage (Ch. 1, line 123):* `"Nineteen days since Lydia Kiefer took a skiff past the breakwater during an October squall and didn't come back."` and `"Lydia Kiefer drowned three days earlier"` (relative to `OCT 17`, fixing the drowning date as **October 14**).
  - *Cross-Reference:* Ch. 2, line 33 explicitly records `"Drowning reported Oct 14, 11:30 PM"` and `"Rosa rugosa — Salt Point, Aug 2026"` (Ch. 2, line 55), and Ch. 10, line 117 explicitly dates Tuesday of the narrative week as **`NOV 3, 2026`**.
  - *Explanation:*
    1. If Day 1 (Monday) is **November 2, 2026**, and the drowning occurred on **October 14, 2026**, the elapsed time from Oct 14 to Nov 2 is exactly **19 days** (`31 - 14 + 2 = 19`). That internal arithmetic (`14 + 3 = 17`; `Oct 14` to `Nov 2` = 19 days) is **internally consistent**.
    2. **However**, in the real-world 2026 calendar, **November 2, 2026 is a Monday**, which means **October 14, 2026 was a Wednesday** (19 days before Monday is ALWAYS a Wednesday: `19 mod 7 = 5` days before Monday = Wednesday). Yet in Ch. 9, line 71, Maren says: `"Lydia walked down my cliff trail Monday afternoon—October twelfth, two days before the gale hit."` If Oct 12 is Monday, then **Oct 14 is Wednesday**, and **19 days later (Nov 2) is indeed Monday!** Wait—let's check the real 2026 calendar!
    3. In real-world 2026: Oct 12, 2026 is a **Monday**! Oct 14, 2026 is a **Wednesday**! Oct 17, 2026 is a **Saturday**! Nov 2, 2026 is a **Monday**! Nov 3, 2026 is a **Tuesday**! Nov 4, 2026 is a **Wednesday**! Nov 5, 2026 is a **Thursday**!
    4. **[CONSISTENT] Verification Result:** The calendar math in Chapter 1 (`Oct 14` Wednesday + 19 days = `Nov 2` Monday; `Oct 14` + 3 days = `Oct 17`) is **100% mathematically and calendar-accurate for the year 2026**!
- **[HIGH] Architectural / Door Lock Mechanism Contradiction (Front Entry Door vs. Mudroom Side Door):**
  - *Passage (Ch. 1, lines 23–25, 35, 95):* `"Before I can knock, the smart-lock deadbolt snaps open on its own. The black door pulls back... The front entry is a low, chilly mudroom tacked onto the main two-story wing... 'The porch keypad rotates codes every two days. Use the brass key for the front door.'"`
  - *Cross-Reference:*
    - In Ch. 1, the front entry *is* the mudroom (`"The front entry is a low, chilly mudroom tacked onto the main two-story wing"`), entered through a single heavy black front door with a rotating porch keypad and a brass key lock.
    - In Ch. 8 (line 57), Cole checks `"the deadbolt throw on the mudroom side door"`.
    - In Ch. 9 (line 9), Tessa exits via `"the mudroom door"`, and in Ch. 10 (line 3), she enters `"through the mudroom side door seconds before Graham's garage bay groans open"`.
    - In Ch. 14 (line 103) and Ch. 21 (line 85) / Ch. 22 (line 21), the front entry with the skylight, coat bench, and tall coat closet is called `"the front vestibule"` and has `"a double-keyed lock beside the sidelight"` (Ch. 22, line 21) requiring a brass key from the *inside* to unlock the deadbolt (`"I dig the brass front-door key out of my jeans with my free hand and toss it across the vestibule to Graham. 'Unlock the deadbolt. Now.' Graham catches it... then turns and works the double-keyed lock beside the sidelight"`), whereas in Ch. 19 (line 39) Cole throws `"the front deadbolt... shut from inside"` without a key (`"followed by the rattle of the front deadbolt throwing shut from inside"`), and in Ch. 1 (line 23) it is a `"smart-lock deadbolt"` that `"snaps open on its own"`.
  - *Explanation:* A deadbolt cannot simultaneously be (a) a motorized smart-lock deadbolt that opens remotely (Ch. 1), (b) a manual thumb-turn deadbolt that Cole rattles shut from inside upon walking in without a key (Ch. 19), and (c) a double-cylinder (`double-keyed`) deadbolt requiring Tessa's brass key from the inside to open for the State Police (Ch. 22). Furthermore, the nomenclature shifts between "front entry / mudroom" (Ch. 1, 4, 6) and "front vestibule" vs. a separate "mudroom side door" / "mudroom sink" (Ch. 7, 8, 10, 14, 15).
  - *Recommended Action:* Clarify in the Location & House Bible and flag for editorial standardization whether the front vestibule and mudroom are one room with two exterior doors (front porch door + side garage/drive door) or two adjacent spaces, and reconcile the front door lock mechanism (smart-lock with interior thumb-turn vs. double-keyed deadbolt).
- **[VERIFY] Historical / Real-World Ferry Service Check (`BAR HARBOR–YARMOUTH — SLIP 4` in October 2026):**
  - *Passage (Ch. 1, line 137):* `"The port ink across the top is smudged: BAR HARBOR–YARMOUTH — SLIP 4. Below it, the date stamp is dry and black. OCT 17."`
  - *Explanation:* The CAT high-speed ferry between Bar Harbor, Maine, and Yarmouth, Nova Scotia historically ends its seasonal passenger service in mid-October (often around Columbus Day / Canadian Thanksgiving, ~Oct 10–15). An October 17 crossing is borderline/plausible in fiction, though worth noting as a minor real-world verification item.

## 4. Logic and Plausibility
- **[MEDIUM] Implausible Cluelessness / Staging Anomaly of the Weekender Bag in the Front Closet:**
  - *Passage (Ch. 1, lines 117–130):* Tessa opens the front entry coat closet immediately after signing the NDA to hang up her wet jacket and finds Lydia's packed weekender (`L.K.`) with the `OCT 17` ferry tag sitting on the floor under Graham's longest charcoal trench coat.
  - *Explanation:* Tessa herself notes the anomaly (`"a man who squares envelopes to table grain doesn't cram a packed weekender behind his coats where a hired cleaner will trip over it night one. Not unless he was in a hurry—or wanted me to find it"`). Later, in Ch. 14 (line 107) and Ch. 17 (line 54), it is revealed that Lydia instructed Cole Halstead to plant the weekender in the front coat closet on Monday morning (`"That wasn't there Sunday night. Halstead came by Monday morning... stood right here in this vestibule for five minutes"`).
  - *Plausibility Issue:* Why would Lydia and Cole—who are framing Graham for **murdering Lydia on October 14** and dumping her body in the cove—plant a packed weekender bag with an **October 17 Bar Harbor–Yarmouth ferry tag** inside Graham's coat closet on Monday morning? An `OCT 17` ferry tag suggests Lydia was *alive and taking a ferry to Nova Scotia three days after her alleged murder*, which undermines a murder frame against Graham rather than reinforcing it! Unless Lydia/Cole wanted to plant evidence that Graham staged a fake runaway trail (or unless the tag was left on by mistake when Cole retrieved the bag, which contradicts Lydia bragging in Ch. 17: `"I told him to drop it there Monday morning"`).
  - *Classification:* **[HIGH]** logical/motivational conflict in antagonist plot logic (see Cross-Chapter Investigation and `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`).
