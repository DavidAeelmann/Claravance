# Chapter 21

Cole's boots hit the kitchen tile. A bright flashlight beam cuts across the black foyer arch and skims the slate inches behind my heels as I pull Graham down the north hall.

"Graham!" Cole yells from the kitchen. His voice bounces off the stone. "Get out here! Think your Boston money gets you off this rock?"

I push Graham into his study and ease the walnut door almost shut. Through the inch-wide crack, the hall stays black. Inside, the smoked-glass server rack hums on its UPS battery backup and casts dull amber light across the desk blotter. The heavy brass pipe wrench I left behind this afternoon still rests beside the keyboard. I don't touch it; I need speed, not dead iron.

Graham leans hard against the wall and catches his breath with one hand pressed to his swollen cheek. "Lydia just threw the generator transfer switch and pulled the manual shutter release in the basement alcove. The hurricane shutters are locked down in their tracks. Without power we can't raise them, and this study door won't hold Cole for ten seconds."

"We're not going out a window," I whisper. I check that the navy duffel is snug across my back and shift the oyster knife in my right hand. "You wired an acoustic house, Graham. Time to use it."

I drop into the desk chair, wake the UPS-backed console, and pull up the *Kiefer Soundworks* zone router. Every upstairs speaker is dead except the basement array, which draws off Zone 4's dedicated battery bank. The exterior cellular bridge went dark with the main power, too. Nothing leaves this house tonight until we physically put the drives in state police hands. One small green status line at the bottom of the screen confirms the boathouse mic puck mirrored onto the Zone 4 RAID array twenty minutes ago. Lydia's confession is waiting down there in the dark.

I highlight the cached `MIC_04_PANTRY_020915.wav` from this afternoon's decrypt, the same recording Lydia knows I copied. I route it to `ZONE_4_DARKROOM_SPEAKERS` and push the gain to the stops. If Cole hears Lydia's voice blasting out of the basement, he won't go door to door upstairs. He'll charge down those concrete steps to kill the playback.

"Stay behind the shelves," I tell Graham, with a jerk of my chin toward the deep walnut stacks in the corner. "If Lydia checks this room, don't breathe, and don't come out until I call you."

I hit `PLAY_LOOP`, then kill the display power so the console keeps running while the room drops back to black. Underneath our feet, muffled through cedar and concrete, Lydia's voice—cool, dry, laughing over the squeak of a wet rag—booms through the basement speakers.

Out in the foyer, Cole's flashlight jerks hard toward the stairwell door. His boots stop dead on the slate.

"Lydie?" he shouts, anger cracking into panic as his own drawl echoes up through the floor. "She's back in Zone Four! She's playing the pantry tape!"

Heavy boots hammer across the foyer toward the basement steps. He takes the concrete landing two treads at a time.

***

I slip out into the dark north hall with my fingers curled around the **B-4** key in my pocket. If I can cross the foyer before Cole reaches the bottom, I can throw the exterior deadbolt on the basement fire door and seal him in. Before I reach the archway, a thin penlight slices across the slate from the living room. Lydia steps out of the shadows with a heavy iron fireplace poker in her grip and cuts into the basement stairwell right behind Cole to tell him the playback is a trap.

With Lydia blocking the foyer stairs, the fire door is out of reach from the hall. The second she tells Cole I triggered the loop from upstairs, he'll smash the Zone 4 RAID array holding her boathouse confession and charge right back up. My only shot at that lock is to flank them from the inside while the speaker blare pulls both of them toward the console.

Lydia's boots clatter down the upper concrete treads behind Cole. I skirt the edge of the hall into the dark kitchen, my hip grazing the island counter, and feel my way into the narrow pantry corridor.

Behind the open spice rack, cold concrete air rises up the dumbwaiter shaft with the dry bite of settled rust dust from the dropped counterweight. I shove the oyster knife into my waistband and hook my forearms over the iron rungs so I don't load my slashed left wrist. Then I drop down fast into the dark cellar while the heavy duffel bangs against the masonry behind my shoulders.

Halfway down, the looped pantry tape swells loud. Lydia's laugh bounces off the concrete, followed by Cole swearing as he hits the open steel fire door of Zone 4. His flashlight slices across the far wall below me and throws long jagged shadows off the server racks.

I drop off the bottom rung onto the rubber matting, knees bent to kill the sound, and back into the charcoal foam wedges of the northeast corner. I wedge myself into the narrow pocket beside the protruding steel frame of the pried-open *ARCHIVE* cabinet. All I need is for Lydia to clear the doorway so I can bolt past their backs into the stairwell.

Graham's dead-zone does its job. The speaker blare still carries through open air, yet the directional baffles kill the reflections off the walls. The sound goes dry and hard to place, and the foam swallows the brush of my wool sweater along with my breathing.

Across the floor, Cole's beam whips over the three humming servers and the worktable. Concrete dust rings the fallen counterweight beside the ladder. Blood has soaked through the gauze wrapped around his left wrist; his right hand grips his duty pistol as he faces the console. He pivots in sharp circles, hunting the speaker that throws his own voice back at him.

"Shut it off!" he roars. He swings the beam toward the desk and kicks a rolling stool out of his way. "Think you can hide down here, you—"

Fast footsteps clatter down the concrete stairs behind him. Lydia bursts through the doorway and sweeps her slim penlight across the darkroom, the wrought-iron fireplace poker raised in her right hand.

"Cole, it's on a loop!" she snaps. "She's not at the desk. She triggered it upstairs!"

I shift my weight an inch to pull the oyster knife from my waistband. The brass zipper on my duffel scrapes lightly against the steel edge of the archive cabinet beside my elbow.

Lydia's penlight snaps into the northeast corner. She doesn't wait for Cole to turn his gun. She charges across the rubber mat, steps right into Cole's line of fire, and swings the iron poker in a level arc at my head.

I drop low into a crouch behind the cabinet's front edge to clear the arc. Moving too fast to check her reach, she overshoots. The iron poker whistles past my ear and slams steel-on-steel into the corner of the archive cabinet with a sharp *CLANG*. A brief flash of sparks jumps off the metal and jars the bar half out of her grip.

Cole spins from the console toward the noise and shouts Lydia's name. His flashlight beam sweeps across her face and blinds her for a split second, while her body still blocks his shot.

I don't wait for a second swing. I yank the oyster knife free, drive forward under Lydia's raised arm, and slash hard across her waxed-canvas sleeve.

She gasps as the rusted edge tears fabric and opens her forearm. The fireplace poker clatters out of her hand onto the concrete, and she stumbles sideways into Cole's chest. They tangle in the dark. Cole's pistol bangs hard off the drafting table while he swears and fights to keep from shooting her in the back.

I cut past both of them, sprint through the open Zone 4 doorway into the stairwell, and catch the exterior handle of the heavy steel fire door.

"Tessa!" Lydia screams behind me as Cole fires one blind shot into the charcoal foam. The crack is deafening in the concrete box.

I haul the inward-swinging steel door back toward the frame, the cold iron handle biting into my palms. Lydia lunges after me and throws herself sideways through the narrowing gap. Her shoulder slams hard against the concrete stairwell wall. A split second later I brace my sneakers on the floor, yank with my whole weight against the handle, and pull the steel flush into the jamb with a heavy *CLANG*. Cole Halstead is trapped inside alone.

I dig the rusted **B-4** key out of my front pocket, jam it into the exterior cylinder, and throw the deadbolt. Steel locks into steel. There is no interior thumb-turn on Cole's side to undo it.

Inside Zone 4, Cole's boots slam furiously against the fire door, his shouts muffled to a dull roar by the foam. With a slashed left wrist, he isn't hauling himself up a vertical iron shaft fast. With the only spare **B-4** key warm in my pocket, that door stays shut until somebody opens it from the hall.

Lydia doesn't turn to fight on the stairs. She scrambles upward ahead of me in the dark, breath ragged. She clamps one hand over her bleeding sleeve and clutches the small portable backup drive she pulled from the study against her ribs.

I take the steps two at a time behind her, the duffel slapping my back. My sneakers skid on the top landing before I shoulder the flush cedar door open into the hall.

Graham is already stepping out of the north corridor and squinting into the dark. Ahead of me, Lydia sprints into the front vestibule. She grabs the heavy cedar coat bench and drags it scraping across the slate right underneath the low skylight hatch.

"Stop her!" I yell. I lunge across the stone as Graham moves to cut her off—his empty hands come up short.

Lydia scrambles onto the bench, unlatches the brass skylight crank with wet, blood-slick fingers, and shoves the heavy glass hatch up into the storm. Freezing rain pours onto the slate and drenches her head and shoulders in a second as she grabs for the wet roof framing.

Then red and blue light bars flare bright through the coastal fog above the spruces. The strobes wash across the open skylight and the wet slate floor while twin sirens cut straight through the gale.
