# Chapter 19

Dusk settles fast over Salt Point and turns the fog around the house a dull, wet gray.

I stay crouched beneath the sunroom sill until cold starts locking up my calves. Inside, Graham is still slumped against the cast-iron radiator, his bruised face pale in the storm light, eyes glued to the front hall arch. He doesn't risk another look at the glass. Every minute I stay out here on the deck, Cole Halstead gets closer up the gravel spur from the causeway.

I back down the deck steps and hug the blind north siding all the way to the front turnaround. Rain drives sideways across the gravel. My wet jeans rub raw against my thighs, and my waterlogged sneakers squelch over pine needles.

My silver Civic is still parked by the spruces where I left it Tuesday night, its trunk lid hovering open in the mist. No cruiser in the drive. Cole left his rig sideways across the causeway throat and hiked back up on foot. Even with a working key fob, there's nowhere left to drive.

I slip onto the front porch under the cedar overhang and duck below the sidelights. Rain hammers the slate steps behind me and covers my breathing. Inside, the vestibule is dark. No movement across the floor, no light spilling from the living room arch. Down the north hall, a floorboard creaks near Graham's study. Lydia must be going through the server rack, or hunting for the raw audio I copied an hour ago before bolting for the cove.

My numb fingertips fumble twice past the multi-tool in my front pocket before catching the brass house key. When I try to line it up with the deadbolt, my thumb has zero pinch left; the key slips and hits the slate sill with a sharp *tink* that freezes me against the door. I scoop it up between both stiff palms, clamp the bow in my knuckles, and lean my shoulder into the turn.

The cylinder rotates with a soft click. I hold my breath, ease the heavy door open a narrow gap, squeeze through, and step into the vestibule. One wet sneaker squeaks on the stone.

I stop dead beside the coat closet and listen. The entry still smells of lemon oil and the brackish puddle Graham swabbed up earlier. Down the north hall, my guest room door stands half-open, my duffel inside with five grand tucked under dry shirts. Nothing moves down there except the steady hum of cooling fans from the study.

I shove the rusted oyster knife into the waistband of my jeans so both hands are free, then cross quietly toward the kitchen arch. The padded manila envelope from the basement lies on the limestone island, its `OCT 14 — MASTER BACKUP` stamp pale in the dim storm light. I leave it where it is. I can't haul a soaked folder down a vertical iron ladder, and pulling Lydia's live boathouse confession off the cellar server is my best shot at exposing both of them.

Halfway across the kitchen tile, a cabinet door clicks shut down the north hall. Through the archway gap, I catch Lydia stepping out of Graham's study and slipping a small portable backup drive into her waxed-canvas pocket.

She's heading my way.

I bolt the last few paces into the pantry corridor, pull the louvered cedar door open a crack, slip inside, and ease it shut behind me. Groping in the dark past the spice shelves I cleared last night, I yank open the unlatched back panel and drop into the dumbwaiter shaft just as a flashlight beam cuts across the kitchen counter.

***

I pull the hinged spice-rack panel flush against its cleat to shut out the hall. Cold air drafts up from the concrete cellar below; it smells of dry cedar dust and old grease. Shivers rack my shoulders while the cut across my left wrist burns against my wet cuff. Out in the kitchen, Lydia's footsteps pause near the pantry corridor, then turn toward the living room.

I feel blindly for the iron ladder bolted to the masonry. My fingers have no grip left, and flexing my slashed left wrist sends a sharp jolt up my forearm. Instead of trusting my hands, I hook the crooks of both elbows over the upper bars, brace my flannel back against the rear hemlock studs, and wedge the rubber edges of my sneakers sideways against the vertical rails as I ease down three rungs to just below the kitchen floor joists. Waterlogged denim drags at my hips; one slip and it will pull my feet right off the rails.

Below me, the muffled hum of the three basement servers rises up the chute. Drop into Zone 4, copy the live boathouse recording onto my thumb drive, climb back up—

Heavy boots hit the front slate overhead, followed by the jingle of a key ring and the rattle of the front deadbolt throwing shut from inside.

Cole is back from the causeway.

"Nothing on the ridge road," his coastal drawl carries down through the joists. "Civic's still out front with the trunk popped. She didn't get off the point, Lydie."

I freeze on the ladder, press my back against the hemlock framing, and lock one trembling knee over an iron bar to take weight off my injured wrist. Cold water drips off my jeans hem into the dark below.

"I'm finishing Graham's note on the tablet now," Lydia says from the kitchen, her heels tapping across tile. "Once I drop it beside him in the sunroom, check the study. She copied the pantry recording from the storm off the tower before she ran for the cove. If she crawled back up those cliff stairs half-drowned, she's going after the basement drive."

"Wrist is bleeding straight through the cruiser gauze," Cole growls, boots thudding toward the island. "Did your father's people actually clear that offshore transfer, or am I bleeding out here on a promise?"

"Once Graham's dead with a signed confession beside him, the governance rider triggers immediate forfeiture. It blocks his estate and moves the remaining three million into my Cayman holding trust," Lydia answers, clipped and impatient. "My father's trustee already has the release queued. Grab Graham's key ring off the study desk, check the basement stairs, and throw the exterior deadbolt on the steel door if she's down there. Let the tide finish it."

Water keeps dripping off my soaked jeans into the concrete well below. If Cole heads down the cellar steps, he'll see the open fire door Graham left unlocked this morning, or spot fresh wet tracks on the rubber mats under his flashlight. I can't outrun a duty pistol inside a padded concrete box, and I can't climb back into the kitchen while both of them stand ten feet from the pantry.

***

I keep my left elbow hooked around an iron rung, wedge both knees and my back hard against the opposite studs for leverage, and reach sideways with my scraped right hand. The cast-iron sheave pulley is bolted to the upper header right above my shoulder. Its braided hemp line runs taut into the side channel, where the pig-iron counterweight hangs suspended over the cellar sill. With the old wooden cab stripped out decades ago, the only thing holding the pulley wheel rigid against that suspended slab of iron is the steel locking pin jammed through the wheel's stop bracket.

Overhead, the flush cedar door under the main stairs creaks open. Heavy boots stop on the top concrete tread.

"Graham's still cuffed to the radiator?" Cole calls from the stairwell.

"I checked the cuffs myself," Lydia answers from the hall. "Get down there and check the server rack. If the boathouse mic puck was live, wipe the platters."

I jam my shoulders harder into the rear studs so my weight hangs off my back and left forearm, hook my stiff right thumb knuckle into the locking pin's ring, and drop my whole weight onto the pull.

Salt rust holds for half a second. Torn skin on my palm flares hot before the steel pin pops free from the bracket.

The braided hemp rope screams across the iron pulley and shakes loose a choking cloud of cedar dust. Inside the recessed side channel, the heavy slab of pig iron plummets onto the concrete sill at the bottom of the shaft.

*BOOM.*

The whole timber chase shudders around my ribs, rattling spice jars behind the pantry shelves.

"What the hell was that?" Cole barks from the stairwell. Heavy boots hammer down the concrete steps two at a time toward the Zone 4 fire door.

"Cole, wait—" Lydia's heels clatter across the foyer toward the basement door. A narrow flashlight beam slices past the kitchen arch, then vanishes down the steps behind him.

I don't wait for the steel door to bang. Muffling a cough in my wet sleeve against the rust dust, I press my shoulder blades flat against the hemlock studs, push upward one rung at a time, and hook my forearms over the iron. I won't trust dead fingers or load my cut wrist. Halfway up, my sneaker slips off wet iron; I clamp my knee into the frame, catch the cedar sill under my chin, and shove the spice-rack panel open.

I haul myself onto the dark pantry floor, ease the louvered door open an inch, and look out. Empty kitchen. Gray storm glare pours through the sunroom arch and catches the pale edge of the manila envelope on the island. Underneath the floorboards, Cole's boots hit concrete.

I pull the rusted oyster knife from my waistband and sprint across the tile toward the sunroom.
