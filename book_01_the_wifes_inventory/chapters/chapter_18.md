# Chapter 18

The brass padlock clatters against the frame. Iron hinges groan as the double door swings wide, and a cold strip of gray storm light cuts across the boathouse floor.

Cole Halstead steps inside with a bundle of thick black zip ties looped over his left forearm and a heavy aluminum Maglite in his right hand. His duty pistol is still holstered. Lydia told him the cleaner was already tied to the piling.

"Lydie, Graham's cuffed to the radiator. Why isn't—"

"Watch her!" Lydia snaps. She scoops the iPad under her left elbow and whips the Glock back toward the teak piling.

I don't wait for the barrel to settle. I drive up off my knees, duck low under the overhang of the bait table, and launch myself across the damp decking before Lydia can clear Cole's shoulder. My sneakers slip on wet moss before the treads bite. Cole turns half a beat too late. His left arm comes up to block me, and I drag the rusted oyster knife hard across the meat of his wrist.

Pitted steel rips through his windbreaker cuff into flesh. Cole curses and staggers backward into the stacked lobster traps as dark blood wells across his knuckles. The Maglite bounces across the planks into the shadows, and the zip ties scatter around his boots. Under the bottom trap, his heavy heel comes down square on my smartphone. The screen cracks loud before the casing skids off the edge into the black slipway water. He clamps his right hand over the gash and blocks the narrow aisle between the bench and the open water.

Lydia's Glock fires, the blast deafening inside the cramped wood box. Cordite cuts through the diesel smell. A chunk of splintered plank blows out beside my left shoulder and peppers my cheek with wet sawdust. Cole's bulk eats half her sightline, and she has the iPad wedged awkwardly under her left elbow; she shoves his shoulder hard to open a lane for a second shot.

I don't look twice at where my phone went. The raw 2:09 AM audio is still on the thumb drive in my watch pocket, and Lydia's own confession just ran up the conduit into the basement server. I spin toward the open slipway gap, tuck my chin, and throw myself sideways into the cove.

The water hits so cold it steals my breath before I can clamp my mouth shut. Salt floods my throat. My soaked jeans and sneakers drag at my hips right away, my chest seizing as I kick for the surface. Above me, boots hammer the boathouse planks. A second shot cracks flat over the water, followed by a dull slap where the slug hits the chop behind my heels.

The outgoing chop pulls hard at my legs and drags me toward the barnacle-crusted pilings. Brine stings deep inside the raw zip-tie cut on my left wrist. I keep my head down and kick hard: fifteen yards, maybe twenty seconds in the water to clear the boathouse shadow before my muscles lock up. My left fingers go numb fast, though my uninjured right hand stays clamped around the oak handle of the oyster knife.

I come up gasping against a jagged shelf of granite downshore toward Maren's sorting shed. Salt spray blinds me, my teeth knocking together as I hook my elbows over the wet ledge to keep my feet out of the chop.

Back at the boathouse, the double doors stand wide open. Cole fills half the frame with his right hand clamped over his bleeding wrist, while Lydia pushes past his shoulder to scan the water. She leans out over the slipway edge for another shot. Cole grabs her shoulder to haul her back from the drop, his boot slides on wet kelp, and his forearm jars her wrist. The matte-black Glock flies clean out of her hand into the churning gap below.

"Damn it, Cole!" she hisses. She stares down into the black water.

"Leave it! She went under the pilings!" Cole shouts over the surf and clamps his hand back over the cut. "Get up to the house and finish Graham's note on that tablet! Surge is taking the causeway. I'll block the road before she crawls out and blows this open!"

I stay plastered against the wet kelp until Lydia disappears up the cliff stairs into the mist. Cole wraps a rag from his belt around his bleeding cuff, mutters under his breath, and heads up toward the drive.

It takes two tries to haul myself onto the granite. My scraped right palm smears pale pink across the wet stone while my slashed left wrist throbs under blue-white skin. I press my palm against my watch pocket and feel the hard plastic ridge of the matte-black thumb drive wedged inside the coin seam, and the brass front-door key still pressed against my thigh in my front pocket. No phone, no dry clothes, and the cold is already creeping past my elbows. North along the cove, sheer granite bluffs cut off the headland, and once high tide tops the causeway nobody walks off Salt Point.

I stumble across crushed oyster shells to Maren's dark sorting shed. The padlock hangs shut, her aluminum skiff chained high on the gravel apron, fresh pickup ruts cutting empty toward Harbor Road. She moved her truck across the culvert before the surge. Still, her outer oyster lines run out from this cove, and a working harvester comes back to the shed to check her gear before full dark.

With my right hand, I fish the wet plasticized tide-chart card out of my watch pocket and pin the stiff coated strip against a cedar clapboard with the heel of my numb left palm. I lock my fingers around the oyster knife and gouge seven jagged words deep into the white plasticized backing: *CALL STATE POLICE — FRAME AT KIEFER HOUSE*. Then I wedge the strip behind Maren's door latch. Behind me, white surf booms against the boathouse pilings. My calf cramps mid-stride as I turn for the cliff stairs.

***

The slate steps feel twice as steep in soaked sneakers and heavy wet denim. Halfway up the bluff, a gust off Penobscot Bay pins me against the wet granite. My scraped palm tears open again on the lichen, and shivering locks both thighs until I drop onto one knee on the slick riser. High above the spruces, an engine revs and tires hiss across wet gravel: Cole's cruiser pulling out of the turnaround and heading south toward the causeway.

My head swims with a quiet, heavy urge to just sit out of the wind for thirty seconds. I force my locked jaw open, count ten steps aloud through chattering teeth, and haul myself upward using my right hand and elbows, one riser at a time.

The light is going fast now, twilight thickening over the headland as I race Lydia's five-fifteen surge. If she and Cole lock the house down before I pull that boathouse recording off the basement server, nobody gets off this point alive to contradict their story. I clear the top tread and drop low behind the sodden junipers lining the back lawn. My ribs ache with every wet breath, and my fingers can barely feel the rusted knife handle pressed against my thigh.

The glass house stands dark in the fog. No lights burn in the living room or the sunroom, and the back porch floodlight flickers and dies as the gale leans into the lines. Through the drizzle, the keypad beside the glass deck door glows a dim amber—useless without a code. I still have the brass front-door key in my pocket, though walking up to the front hall means walking blind into Lydia.

I sprint low across the wet grass and climb onto the cedar deck beside the sunroom. The heavy glass slider I left wide open is shut. Its brass thumb-latch is turned from inside—Cole must have locked it after he cuffed Graham and headed down to the cove. I test the handle gently, careful not to rattle the track. Solid. I press my shoulder against the siding, wipe salt spray off my lashes with a soaking sleeve, and peer through the rain-streaked glass.

Inside, the room is dim and empty in the gray storm light bleeding through the ocean panes. The gray wool rug still lies crooked over the bleached boards where Graham dragged it back this morning. Past it, Graham Kiefer slumps sideways against the interior wall under the cast-iron baseboard radiator. His left wrist is cuffed to the iron pipe. A dark bruise swells under his left eye, and his charcoal sweater hangs torn at the collar where Cole hauled him out of the study.

As my shadow crosses the glass, Graham's head snaps up. He spots me, shakes his head hard toward the front hall, and taps his free hand silently against his chest in warning. Lydia is inside, probably waiting past the kitchen arch with a kitchen knife or Graham's pipe wrench to watch the glass for my outline.

I drop below the sill and glance south across the inlet toward the mainland road.

White foam boils across the low asphalt of the causeway and swallows the steel guardrails in gray-green chop. Cole Halstead's sheriff's cruiser is parked dead sideways across the throat of the flooded road, its light bar dark, sealing both lanes from shoulder to shoulder. Between the cruiser, the rising tide, and the cliffs, nobody's driving in—and nobody's driving out.
