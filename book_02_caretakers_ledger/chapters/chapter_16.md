# Chapter 16

I don't move until Hollis's boots haul Sloane across the shattered glass toward the front foyer, shouting at Pike through the side sidelight.

I back-crawl away from the gallery balusters, keeping my belly flat against the cold floorboards until I clear the center joists, then slip silently back inside the west linen closet. Two minutes later I'm back up the hot brick pipe chase inside **Room 4 — Attic East**, my chest heaving against the dry heat as I tell Maya what happened below.

"Hollis shot out the chandelier," I whisper, keeping my hand pressed over my bruised shoulder while I catch my breath. "He's got Sloane at gunpoint, and Celeste is cornered behind the lectern. If Sloane misses her six-A.M. satellite check-in, Boston sends State Police—unless Hollis forces her to clear the check-in first."

Maya's fingers tighten around the oilskin-wrapped **Caretaker's Ledger**. "And the Iridium chip?"

"Lying on the flagstones beside the lectern," I tell her. "Right next to Sloane's spilled briefcase. If I can pull that transceiver and her satellite uplink key before Hollis sweeps the hearth, we can queue an emergency packet ourselves."

"Wait until they settle," Maya says softly, her eyes dark in the red penlight glow. She tears a fresh strip of clean gauze from the roll beside her thermos and wraps it firmly around my raw, soot-stained right palm so the skin won't stick to the freezing iron rungs. "Don't go down into the hall while Hollis still has a live shell in both barrels. And Clara—if I hear a shot downstairs and you don't come back up the shaft within ten minutes, I'm not sitting here waiting for dawn."

"Stay behind the cedar louver until you know where Hollis is," I say, squeezing her shoulder. "Keep the ledger inside your shirt."

We wait in the stifling dark behind the louver until **1:15 AM**, watching the luminous hands crawl around my watch dial while the wind rattles the east gable slate overhead. My bruised shoulder stiffens against the warm brick, and every breath tastes of dry plaster dust.

Below us, the house finally settles into a tense, watchful hush. A heavy oak bar slides across the front porch deadbolt, followed by the metallic scrape of Hollis dragging a heavy dining table against the kitchen veranda door. Twice, Hollis's boots cross the Great Hall flagstones to check the dining-room shutters, testing every latch before the lower floor goes quiet. Down in the Great Hall, a single oil lamp burns low on the mantel, casting thin shadows while Sloane sits silent by the dying coals.

I check the heavy brass **ROOM 4 — ATTIC EAST** tag inside my breast pocket—its sharp stamped corner pressing against my ribs—slide down the vertical pipe chase past the second floor, and drop all the way to the **butler's pantry dumbwaiter shaft**. My boots land lightly on the wooden roof of the dumbwaiter car, which sags slightly on its frayed hemp pulley rope.

The narrow wooden car hangs motionless at the bottom of the well, its shelves still smelling of damp hemp and old hide glue from Maya's Wednesday raid. I unlatch the lower dumbwaiter hatch an inch, listen to the dead quiet of the dark kitchen, smell stale brine and cold linoleum, and slip out onto the scuffed pine floor. The refrigerator compressor clicks off in the corner, leaving the room silent except for the wind rattling the storm shutters. Near the pantry swing-door, the heavy dining table Hollis dragged against the veranda glass blocks any direct exit into the yard.

***

Before risking the Great Hall flagstones, I creep down the narrow cellar stairs behind the pantry, my boots finding the worn edges of the pine treads so they don't creak.

The **sub-basement boiler vault** hums with steady mechanical heat, the concrete floor vibrating faintly beneath my damp boots while a sharp trace of spent cordite hangs near the shattered brickwork. My shoulders ache from the climb down the shaft, and my knees feel ready to buckle. A bare yellow bulb still burns above the workbench, illuminating the broken mortar around the coal-chute mouth and the spilled pile of gray clinkers on the floor. Overhead, the hot copper riser feeding Room 4 ticks steadily as the circulator pump pushes water up three stories.

I crouch beside the cast-iron **ash-pit damper**. The heavy vertical baffle sits slightly bound in its steel side tracks where buckshot chewed the mortar, and the recessed gravity counterweight sits half-wedged in its vertical grease slot. On the brass release lever outside the slot, a fresh greasy smudge catches the yellow bulb light—where Hollis checked the latch after shooting into the chute. Using my steel multi-tool pliers, I clear two crushed brick chips out of the vertical track, test the pin's travel until it slides smooth, and loop a short length of copper bell-wire from the workbench around the counterweight pin—leaving the baffle rigged on a hairline release so one sharp pull on the brass lever outside will drop the heavy cast-iron plate straight across the chute throat.

Behind the furnace, a short concrete stair rises to the **exterior cellar bulkhead**, its angled timber doors barred from the inside by a heavy wrought-iron crossbar dropped into steel brackets. Snow packs silently against the frosted glass light-well beside the frame. Knowing that inside bar is seated—and knowing how easily someone could wedge it from outside in the snow—tells me this basement isn't an exit if the kitchen stairs get cut off.

Next, I cross to the oil drum rack beside the stairs, note the red float sitting near the bottom quarter of the tank's sight glass, and lift a spare, unopened **gallon tin of lamp kerosene**—the same high-flash fuel Hollis used to burn the clinical manifests in the hearth Wednesday night. The tin sloshes heavy against my hip as I carry it quietly up the cellar steps and stash it inside the dark broom alcove just inside the pantry swing-door—tucking the sealed evidence baggie with Celeste's charred spray canister, the crystal brandy stopper, the folded *VaneCor* strips, and the Survivorship Covenant behind the tin so I don't lose them if Hollis corners me in the hall.

My gauze-wrapped right hand is gray with coal soot and grease. I wipe my knuckles on my wool trousers, step silently up to the cracked pantry swing-door, and peer through the narrow hinge gap into the **Great Hall**.

The room sits half-dark and silent, lit only by the dying birch coals and the low oil lamp on the mantel.

In the worn leather armchair beside the hearth, **Sloane Mercer-Hall** sits upright in her camel hair coat, her ankles bound to the chair legs with white nylon rope and her wrists lashed behind the wooden slats. A dark bruise swells high on her left cheekbone where Hollis slammed her against the stone. Her leather briefcase lies open and empty on the hearth rug beside broken chandelier glass. Near the front foyer, Hollis's heavy boots crunch softly over stray glass shards as he checks the porch sidelights, his back turned toward the study archway.

Celeste isn't in sight.

Ten paces to my left, resting on the corner of the oak reception lectern beside the fallen Guest Ledger, the tiny amber diode on Sloane's **Iridium satellite burst-transceiver** blinks once in the gloom—right next to a silver key-fob uplink token lying half-buried in shattered chandelier crystal.

And sitting just inside the dark **study archway** rests my **faded navy canvas duffel**.

***

I wait until Hollis's boots step into the front vestibule to test the exterior storm-door latch, his heavy shoulder blocking the faint exterior porch light.

I ease the pantry door open just wide enough to slip through, keeping my boots along the wool runner rug so I don't crunch a single shard of leaded glass. My heart hammers hard against my ribs. Every step across the flagstones feels exposed under the high dark rafters, where the broken chandelier chains sway slightly in the hearth draft.

I reach the oak reception lectern, scoop the blinking **Iridium transceiver** and Sloane's silver **satellite uplink fob** off the wood with my gauze-wrapped hand, and drop both straight into my right coat pocket beside my multi-tool. The cold silver fob clinks faintly against the brass skeleton key, and I freeze for a full second until Hollis's boots scrape again on the vestibule tile.

Across the hearth, Sloane's eyes lift from the dying birch coals and lock straight onto mine. Her mouth stays pressed shut, her pale face gleaming faintly with sweat in the low lamp glow.

She doesn't gasp. She doesn't call out to Hollis. Her silver-streaked head tilts half an inch toward the dark study archway behind me—a silent, cold warning that comes half a heartbeat too late.

I pivot toward the study archway, grab the canvas strap of my **navy duffel** off the worn leather armchair, and check the open zipper—my sliced **$45,000 NDA portfolio** is still inside, minus the transceiver now warming in my pocket. The leather wallet and spare cash I stashed in the side pocket haven't been touched. I step backward toward the pantry shadow, hauling the duffel tight against my hip while my boots brush the edge of the braided wool rug.

Behind my right ear, a loose oak floorboard gives a tiny, sharp creak, followed by the soft rustle of cashmere against dry paneling.

I try to duck, raising my left forearm to shield my temple.

**Celeste Vane** steps out of the pitch-black blind spot behind the walnut bookcase and swings Arthur's heavy **brass desk weight** in a tight, vicious arc.

The solid brass pyramid clips the side of my left shoulder and glances hard off the bone behind my ear. White sparks explode across my vision, and warm blood trickles down inside my wool collar. My knees buckle instantly, dropping me hard onto the flagstone floor as my navy duffel spills across the rug.

Before I can roll onto my side or reach the multi-tool in my coat pocket, heavy Vibram boots slam down beside my ribs, pinning my coat hem to the stone. The brass desk weight clatters onto the floor beside my temple, still warm from Celeste's palm.

"Got her," Celeste hisses, breathing fast and ragged in the dark as she steps back toward the study doorway, wiping her sweating palms on her cashmere sleeve.

Cold steel presses tight behind my right ear as **Hollis Greer** steps out of the vestibule shadow with the twelve-gauge leveled at my face, his thumb clicking the safety off while he reaches into his chore-coat pocket with his scarred right hand for a fistful of thick black nylon **zip ties**.
