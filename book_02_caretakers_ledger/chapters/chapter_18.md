## Chapter 18

Scalding white steam hits my cheeks like a wet wool blanket, stinging the raw scrape along my jaw.

Wet birch ash fills the air, thick and greasy, choking every breath down to a shallow rasp. Behind me, the iron grate hisses violently as cistern water continues to pour down the stone chimney flue, turning the Great Hall hearth into a black, boiling cauldron. Nobody can see across the flagstones.

I drop the wrought-iron log tongs onto the carpet, grab Margot's camel hair sleeve with my left hand, and haul her low through the blown-open study doorway into the blinding white fog. The heavy walnut chair Hollis wedged under the knob skids sideways across the flagstones and clatters against the reception lectern. Nobody can see two paces ahead through the boiling cloud, and the roar of steam from the chimney flue swallows the sound of our boots.

I drop blindly to my knees beside the stone hearth apron, feel the hot steam draft across the nape of my neck, and sweep my palms across the wet slate until my split index knuckle strikes cold steel. My **steel multi-tool**, the cracked **Iridium satellite burst-transceiver**, Margot's silver uplink fob, and my keys sit scattered right where Simone dumped them beside the boots. Every second counts before Hollis or Simone recovers from the blast. Before shoving the gear deep into my right coat pocket, I jam my thumb hard onto the silver fob's recessed panic button—holding it down until a single faint amber LED blinks once through the soot, firing a blind SOS burst toward Boston. My split knuckle stings sharply against the cold metal keys, and I leave the sodden yellow legal pad behind in the wet gray birch ash.

Beside me, Margot coughs violently into her camel hair collar, stumbling against the edge of the hearth apron as she strains against the white nylon rope around her elbows. "Tessa—he's got a backup revolver in his chore coat. Get out of the hall."

"Stay down behind the heavy leather wing," I whisper, shoving her low behind the tall fireside armchair before I can even draw my folding pocket blade to cut her ropes.

A shadow lunges out of the gray fog toward my left shoulder, swinging something heavy and square—Arthur's brass desk weight.

Simone. I know her stride before I even see her face.

I drop flat onto one palm, scoop a double handful of freezing, soot-thick birch slurry straight off the hearth apron, and fling it upward into the dark gap where her cashmere collar meets the steam.

The wet soot catches her square across the eyes. Simone shrieks, stumbling backward over the fallen chandelier chains and crashing hard into the slanted oak lectern, sending the heavy brass desk weight clattering across the flagstones.

"Hollis!" she screams, clawing blindly at her face as wet gray ash streaks down her turtleneck. "She's loose—she's heading for the study!"

Heavy Vibram boots scrape across broken chandelier glass near the hearth. Two crisp metallic clicks echo through the steam as Hollis snaps the breech shut on fresh buckshot shells in his twelve-gauge.

***

I don't run toward the front oak door—Pike's county plow is idling somewhere outside in the turnaround, and the porch steps are an open shooting gallery.

Instead, I stay low beneath the drifting steam bank and cut left past the study archway toward the **mudroom coat rack**, where **Arthur Cutler's custom walnut orthopedic cane** still leans untouched against the cedar paneling. My fingers close around the curved brass handle. Heavy hardwood, weighted at the **unworn rubber ferrule** that proved Arthur never walked out of this lodge into Tuesday's blizzard—and solid enough to break bone.

Behind me, the twelve-gauge roars inside the Great Hall.

Wood splinters explode off the oak lectern frame, showering my coat back with stinging chips as buckshot tears through the empty leather armchair where I knelt moments earlier. Margot lets out a muffled curse from the floor beside the hearthstone, unhit, as the concussion rattles the mounted stag heads along the timber walls.

I shove through the swinging baize door into the **commercial kitchen**.

Cold tile meets my boots. Pale silver frost-glare spills through the tall pantry window, catching the row of heavy copper saucepans hanging above the butcher-block island while wet soot drifts in from the hall behind me. Against the far wall, the massive black cast-iron range radiates faint residual warmth from the pilot manifold, while the walk-in cold larder door stands latched beside the pastry counter.

I slip behind the stainless-steel prep table and edge toward the **butler's pantry** doorway, gripping Arthur's walnut cane with both hands like a batter's bat. The steel counter chills my hip through my wet wool coat. Beyond the baize swing-door, Simone is still coughing violently in the Great Hall, screaming at Hollis not to leave her alone in the dark with Margot.

The kitchen swing-door bangs wide open against the plaster wall, rattling a rack of iron skillets along the brick chimney breast.

Hollis steps across the threshold, coughing hard from the birch smoke, his scarred right hand locked around the shotgun's trigger guard while his left sleeve wipes gray ash from his eyes. His heavy Vibram soles crunch over dry coarse salt spilled from the larder shelf. For two heartbeats he pauses beside the cast-iron range, listening in the dark, his wintergreen breath ragged in the quiet kitchen.

He catches my silhouette shifting against the frosted pantry glass and swings the twin-barrels up.

I step inside his reach before the muzzle clears the island corner and swing **Arthur's weighted walnut cane** upward with every ounce of shoulder I have left.

The heavy hardwood shaft catches the shotgun's wooden fore-end square against the steel under-lug, driving the barrel skyward just as Hollis's finger jerks the trigger.

The deafening blast blows three copper saucepans clean off their iron hooks overhead, raining brass and plaster dust across the stainless-steel counter and stinging my cheeks with hot grit. Before Hollis can break the breech, I bring the brass-handled cane down across the **wooden shotgun stock** where it meets his hip—once, twice—cracking the walnut grip clean through at the wrist pin against the oak pantry doorjamb.

Hollis grunts hard as the splintered stock tears out of his scarred right hand, sending the jammed barrel clattering across the kitchen flagstones. He lunges blindly with his left fist, catching the edge of my wool collar before I twist free, kick the heavy butcher-block stool across his shins, and slam the pantry door shut between us.

***

"Tessa!"

The wooden **dumbwaiter hatch** in the pantry wall slides upward with a dry scrape of hemp rope.

**Nina** drops out onto the pine floorboards, landing hard on her laced leather boots and biting back a sharp gasp against the pain in her side. Her dark hair sticks damply to her forehead, and buttoned tight inside her heavy flannel shirt sits the oilskin-wrapped **Caretaker's Ledger**.

I catch her by the elbow before her knees buckle, steadying her against the cold larder shelves. "You flooded the flue," I breathe, feeling the feverish heat radiating off her gray wool sweater.

"I heard Hollis's voice in the chimney chase," Nina whispers, pressing her ink-stained palm against her bruised side as she glances toward the shaking kitchen door. Her breath whistles thinly through clenched teeth. "He's got a snub-nose revolver in his chore coat—I saw him load it in the barn Tuesday night. We can't go out the front."

I reach into the dark **broom alcove** beside the dumbwaiter shaft, shove the stashed evidence baggie and Covenant back into my coat pocket, and haul out the spare **gallon tin of lamp kerosene** I left there an hour ago, hooking my fingers through the cold wire bail. "Sub-basement," I tell her, nodding toward the narrow cellar stair door behind the flour bins while shifting Arthur's walnut cane into my left hand. "I rigged the ash-pit counterweight on the coal chute. We climb out through the stone icehouse."

I shove the heavy oak flour chest sideways across the pantry door to buy us a few extra heartbeats, then pull the cellar latch open. Dry furnace heat and old coal dust rise from the steep wooden steps below.

Heavy boots slam twice against the pantry door behind us, splintering the pine latch housing. Metal scrapes against oak as Hollis jams a pry-bar or his revolver butt into the frame, his voice muffled and hoarse through the thick timber.

Before the door gives way, three rapid **gunshots** crack across the snowbound forecourt—panicked revolver rounds fired straight through the front porch sidelights toward the shadows moving inside the hall, followed instantly by the high, frantic roar of a heavy diesel engine redlining in low gear.

I yank the frosted pantry window shutter open an inch and look out across the moonlit drift. Freezing wind needles my eyes, carrying the sour bite of unburned exhaust.

**Deputy Enoch Pike** leaps back into the cab of his idling county plow truck, wheeling the massive yellow blade wildly across the iced turnaround after hearing the shotgun blasts inside the lodge. His amber roof strobe sweeps erratically across the black spruce walls.

"He's panicking," Nina breathes beside my shoulder, gripping the edge of the pantry shelf. "He thinks Hollis is cleaning house and he's next on the list."

Pike slams the heavy snowplow truck straight into reverse, loses traction on the glazed ice sheet beside the rear drive, and guns the throttle forward in blind terror to clear the narrow lane—ramming the steel plow wing **straight through the double timber doors of the woodshed and exterior fuel-transfer bay** beside the kitchen wing.

Pine planks explode outward in a spray of splinters and torn brass hardware, exposing the auxiliary red-dyed diesel reserve tank inside the shed while brushing the tail of the nearest snowmobile outside. The heavy iron padlock snaps off its staple and skitters across the snow.

As the plow blade shears past the kitchen foundation, the steel corner rips clean through the **exterior copper diesel reserve line** feeding the sub-basement tank. Raw red-dyed fuel sprays sideways across the snowbank in a thick, glistening arc, hissing loudly in the subzero air as orange sparks shower off the torn iron hasp beneath Pike's spinning rear tires. An instant later, a dull blue-orange *whump* catches along the soaked pine siding, sending greasy black diesel smoke rolling toward the lodge's rear eaves. Fire races straight toward the kitchen wing.
