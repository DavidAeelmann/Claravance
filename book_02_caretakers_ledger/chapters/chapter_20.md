## Chapter 20

Subzero night air hits my soot-caked lungs like crushed ice as Nina hauls me up over the brick lip of the **stone icehouse**.

We collapse side by side onto the frozen rock salt beside the north granite wall. Just beyond our boots, beneath a half-melted drift of sawdust and burlap, **Arthur Cutler's** rigid silhouette lies exactly where I found him hours ago—still wearing his thin dress socks, his swollen left toe stiff under the salt crust. Greasy black diesel smoke curls upward from the brick coal chute behind us, and the smell of burning pine shingles hangs sharp over the damp salt air. Even in the dark, the contrast is brutal—scalding smoke rising from below while freezing wind cuts down through the burned-out gable vent above, turning the sweat on my collar into thin ice.

"Up," I rasp, catching Nina's elbow and nudging her toward the splintered cedar rafters where the timber gable vent burned through above the door during Simone's arson attempt before the subzero sleet and thick roof snow smothered the petrol flames. "Through the vent frame—before the smoke fills the pit and chokes us out."

Nina bites down hard against the pain in her side and climbs first, her laced leather boots finding purchase on the frosted granite blocks and charred timber lath. Twice a loose slate shingle slides beneath her heel, clattering back down onto the rock salt beside Arthur's corpse. She squeezes through the jagged **icehouse roof vent** opening into the open drift, gasping softly as she hits the crusted snow outside, then turns back to grip my wool collar and haul me clear of the soot-blackened beams.

We drop knee-deep into crusted powder behind the kitchen wing. Frost immediately stiffens my wet coat sleeves, and the sudden subzero wind cuts straight through the sweat on my neck. Behind us, the stone icehouse vents thick gray coal smoke into the pine canopy like a broken chimney.

Ahead across the snowbound forecourt, the **woodshed and exterior fuel-transfer bay** behind the kitchen wing are a roaring torch of orange diesel flame, sending thick black smoke billowing across the white spruce canopy. Sparks whirl upward into the freezing dark like red insects. The fire has already jumped the eaves onto the rear kitchen roof, turning the frosted upper rafters orange. Further down the drive, Deputy Pike's county snowplow sits nose-deep in the frozen ditch, its amber roof strobe still sweeping uselessly across empty snow—and Pike himself nowhere in sight near the cab, probably scrambling down the logging road on foot to save his own neck.

Near the front porch steps, the headlights of **Margot's black armored SUV** flare abruptly to life, cutting twin white beams through the drifting ash. Heavy studded snow tires crunch softly as the suspension settles under someone's weight.

Simone.

She stands beside the open driver's door in her soot-streaked cashmere turtleneck and winter boots, clutching a black leather medical case packed with *CutlerCor* vials and a thick brass-tagged binder under her left arm. She must have snatched Margot's spare key fob off the mudroom console before locking Hollis in the basement—leaving Margot tied inside the Great Hall while the kitchen joists burned.

She tosses the medical case onto the passenger seat, climbs behind the wheel, and reaches for the push-button start, her white-gloved fingers shaking violently in the dashboard glow.

***

"Nina—stay behind the stone pillar!" I shout, sprinting straight across the glazed ice of the turnaround with **Arthur's walnut cane** gripped tight in my gloved hand. Freezing wind cuts through my torn coat sleeves, stinging the fresh scrapes on my wrists.

My boots slide twice on the packed slush. I catch my balance against the rear quarter-panel just as the heavy V8 engine turns over with a low, throaty rumble, exhaust pluming thick and white into the orange-lit snow.

Simone slams the driver's door shut—or tries to.

I drive the **brass handle of Arthur's walnut cane** straight into the closing door gap, jamming the heavy armored steel frame wide against the latch striker. The walnut shaft groans under the impact, though the hardwood holds.

Simone shrieks and throws her shoulder against the door to force it shut, reaching with her right hand for the console gearshift lever. "Get off the car, Tessa! You're dead if you stay up here!"

I wedge my hip into the door gap, yank the **steel multi-tool** out of my coat pocket with my right hand, and flip open the needle-nose pliers. Beneath the left side of the steering column, the black plastic **kick-panel fuse cover** pops free with one sharp tug of my boot toe, exposing neat rows of colored blade fuses and square relays. Cold leather smells of espresso and damp cashmere inside the heated cab.

Simone grabs a heavy brass flashlight off the center console and swings it straight at my temple.

I duck inside the arc, taking the blow across my left shoulder blade. White pain flares down my arm, nearly numbing my fingers, yet my pliers lock cleanly onto the square **ignition relay** inside the fuse block. My split index finger slips once against the steel grip, smearing warm blood across the plastic housing before I clamp down harder.

I twist and yank.

The plastic relay snaps free in my steel jaws with a faint dry crack. Instantly, the V8 engine cuts dead, the headlights wink out, and the dashboard goes black—leaving only the pulsing orange glow of the burning woodshed reflecting off the windshield and the cold click of cooling exhaust pipes beneath the chassis.

"Give me the keys," I pant, gripping Simone's cashmere wrist and hauling her half out onto the running board before she can reach the console again. I snatch Margot's spare key fob from the cup holder with my left hand and fling it sideways into the deep snowbank behind the porch steps. "And leave the *CutlerCor* case on the seat. Nobody is driving down that logging road tonight, and nobody is burying Arthur's death until the state police walk through that study."

Simone kicks wildly at my shins with her leather boot, her pale hair whipping across her soot-streaked cheeks. "You think you won?" she spits, grabbing at the door pillar to keep from hitting the snow. "You're standing in the middle of nowhere with a dead satellite tracker and zero cell bars! Even if you survive tonight, Margot Kiefer-Hall will bury your sister's ledger under six layers of federal injunctions before noon—and the thirty-eight million still vests to me at five! Nobody's coming up that road before Saturday!"

I reach into my right coat pocket with my free hand and hold the sealed **zip-top evidence baggie**—containing the wrapped **crystal brandy stopper** and the **half-charred silver nitroglycerin rescue-spray canister**—straight in front of her face. With my thumb, I pin the folded **three-page Survivorship Covenant** against the plastic baggie so she sees every piece of her timeline at once.

In the orange firelight from the burning woodshed, the faceted glass inside the clean plastic still gleams faintly with **compounded lavender cuticle oil**—the exact signature of the woman who stood beside Arthur's study desk at eleven-forty Tuesday night while his walnut cane sat untouched in the mudroom rack. The sharp medicinal scent of the burning diesel bay hangs thick around us.

"Not after the state medical examiner swabs this stopper and tests the residue inside your charred spray canister," I say, keeping my voice steady over the roar of the fire. "I pulled your burned *CutlerCor* blister packs out of your vanity porcelain bowl, Simone. Nina watched you press that tampered nozzle under Arthur's tongue at eleven-forty Tuesday night. You poisoned your husband on his own study rug, packed him in salt to fake his time of death, and then wedged an iron bar across the cellar bulkhead to burn Hollis alive."

Simone's pale fingers go rigid against the door frame. Her shoulders shake in the freezing draft as the truth settles over her—Nina didn't die in the snow, and the evidence didn't burn in the hearth. She stares at the sealed crystal stopper in my palm, then past my shoulder toward the side of the burning lodge.

Her eyes widen in pure, unmasked terror.

***

A deafening crash of shattering glass and splintered cedar sash erupts from the **frosted cellar light-well** beside the kitchen foundation. Thick shards of frosted pane scatter across the orange-lit snowbank, followed by a gout of greasy black diesel smoke pouring out of the basement gap.

I spin sideways, shoving Simone hard against the SUV's rear fender just as a dark, soot-blackened figure heaves himself upward out of the broken basement window frame into the snowdrift. Simone drops onto one knee in the slush behind the rear tire, scrambling backward out of his sightline with her hands pressed over her mouth.

**Hollis.**

His wool chore coat sleeve is ripped clean to the elbow where he tore his crushed **left wrist** free from the cast-iron ash baffle—leaving a jagged trail of dark blood across the white snowbank as he rolls to his feet in the firelight. His left arm hangs limp and twisted against his ribs, slick with wet kerosene, ash, and freezing blood, yet his scarred right hand still grips the steel **snub-nose revolver**. He wheezes harshly in the subzero air, every breath rattling wetly in his soot-choked chest as steam curls off his torn coat shoulders.

He doesn't look at Simone crouching behind the wheel well. He doesn't even glance at the burning woodshed behind him, where orange flames are already licking into the spruce boughs and sending burning pine needles drifting across the forecourt.

His bloodshot eyes lock straight onto **Nina**, who has stepped half out from behind the granite gate pillar beside the drive with the oilskin-wrapped **Caretaker's Ledger** clutched tight against her chest. She doesn't drop the book, and she doesn't back away into the spruce. Her pale face is lit bright orange by the burning rafters, her breath hanging in thin white plumes against the dark timber.

"Leave her alone, Hollis!" I shout, stepping sideways off the SUV running board to draw his aim toward me, my fingers tightening on Arthur's walnut cane.

Orange firelight glints off the missing joint of his scarred right hand. Hollis ignores me completely. He staggers several steps forward through the crusted drift, boots crunching loud in the frozen quiet between the SUV and the woodshed, and raises the snub-nose revolver across the snowlit hood of Margot's truck—aiming straight at Nina's heart while frost glints cold along the steel barrel and his scarred right hand tightens on the iced grip.
