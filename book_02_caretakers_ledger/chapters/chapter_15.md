# Chapter 15

By ten-forty-five, the voices downstairs grow sharper, carrying clearly up the dry air of the brick pipe chase behind Room 4.

Maya stays tucked inside the warm attic berth with the oilskin-wrapped **Caretaker's Ledger** buttoned inside her flannel shirt, refusing to let the book anywhere near the ground floor while Sloane is inside the house. She grips my wrist once before I slide back into the shaft, her ink-stained thumb pressing hard against my healing scar. Her palms feel dry and fever-warm against my skin.

"Don't let Sloane see you," Maya whispers, her breath catching lightly against her taped ribs. "She didn't drive four hours in a snowstorm to negotiate. And watch the second-floor gallery planks near the center rail—they groan if you put your full weight on them."

I nod once, lower myself rung by rung down the steel plumbing straps in total darkness, and slip out through the second-floor linen closet hatch. The corridor outside sits pitch-black and freezing, smelling of dry cedar dust and cold plaster. A faint trace of Celeste's lavender oil still hangs near the West Wing archway, layered under the sharp tang of woodsmoke rising from the Great Hall hearth below.

I stay flat on my belly as I crawl onto the dark **second-floor gallery balcony**, keeping my weight distributed along the heavy wall joists so the pine boards don't pop. I edge behind a row of heavy oak balusters directly above the fieldstone fireplace. Beneath my wool coat sleeves, a recessed **cast-iron floor register** opens straight down through the timber ceiling joists into the Great Hall, channeling rising birch-smoke warmth—and every word spoken around the hearth—straight to my ear.

Through the ornate iron scrollwork, I look down on three people standing inside a tight circle of yellow firelight beneath the mounted stag heads and blackened timber rafters. Woodsmoke curls thick from the birch logs, mingling with the damp wool smell of wet winter coats.

**Sloane Mercer-Hall** hasn't even taken off her tailored camel hair coat. She stands beside the reception lectern with her leather briefcase resting against her shin, her silver-streaked bob neat and dry despite the snow outside. Faint steam rises from the damp wool hem of her trousers near the hearth. Across the flagstone floor, **Hollis Greer** leans his soot-blackened hip against the mantel with his twelve-gauge shotgun angled toward the floor, chewing wintergreen tobacco with slow, deliberate jaw strokes. **Celeste Vane** stands five paces to Hollis's left in her black cashmere turtleneck, her pale arms crossed tight over her chest as she watches Sloane like a hawk.

"Let's drop the probate theater, Celeste," Sloane says, her Boston cadence crisp and unhurried in the high-raftered room. "I didn't come up here for a ten-day inventory. I know Arthur didn't ride a snowmobile into a blizzard Tuesday night. He had acute gout in his left foot and couldn't walk fifty paces across flat ground without his walnut cane."

Celeste's chin lifts a fraction. "Arthur left the house on his own, Sloane. Hollis and I were in the carriage barn."

"Arthur didn't answer his scheduled nine-o'clock fiduciary call on Wednesday morning," Sloane replies without raising her voice. "You told my receptionist he and Celeste took the snowmobiles down to the lower ridge cabin before the lines went down, Hollis—hoping to stall me until Saturday. Except I had an anonymous fax on my desk before dawn, and my investigator's satellite tracker has been pinging inside this house since yesterday dusk. Nobody's buying a blizzard walk-off so Arthur's voting shares can vest to you at five o'clock tomorrow."

***

Hollis spits a dark stream of wintergreen juice straight into the birch embers, where it hisses against the hot stone.

"Your investigator's dead, Miss Mercer-Hall," Hollis says flatly, his scarred right hand shifting along the shotgun fore-end. "She went down the icehouse coal chute two hours ago when the whole timber roof went up in petrol. You're welcome to dig through the ash pit tomorrow morning if you want what's left of her."

My chest tightens against the cold oak floorboards.

Sloane doesn't flinch. She doesn't even glance toward the kitchen door. Instead, she nods once toward the worn leather armchair beside Arthur's study archway—where my **faded navy canvas duffel** sits unzipped on the seat cushion, exactly where Hollis dumped it after sweeping my second-floor guest room.

"Did she have her navy duffel with her in the icehouse, Hollis?" Sloane asks calmly.

"Pulled it out of her closet myself," Hollis grunts. "Nothing in it but wool socks and a leather probate folder. Same as the encrypted tablet she had magnet-taped behind her Civic's wheel well down at the gate. Pike and I tore the mount off an hour before you climbed the ridge."

Sloane walks across the flagstones, lifts the leather **$45,000 NDA portfolio** out of my open navy duffel, and flips it open on the oak reception lectern beside the sliced Guest Ledger. She pulls a slender silver penknife from her camel hair coat pocket, slices cleanly down the stitched leather spine of the portfolio, and peels back the heavy calfskin lining.

Wedged inside a routed foam hollow sits a flat, thumb-sized matte-black circuit board wired to a lithium coin cell, blinking a tiny amber diode once every four seconds. My throat goes dry. Sloane didn't just buy my debt—she wired a live satellite tracker straight into the leather spine I carried into my own sister's hiding place.

"An **Iridium satellite burst-transceiver**," Sloane says, holding the black chip up between two gloved fingers so the firelight catches the copper antenna trace. "Immune to your dead cell grid, and accurate to six feet. It pinged from the second-floor west guest room until four-thirty this afternoon, when it moved straight downstairs into Arthur's study—which tells me you moved her bag, Hollis, and it also tells me she left that portfolio behind before she bolted."

Hollis's jaw goes rigid, the muscle bunching beneath his gray stubble. He stares at the blinking black chip in Sloane's fingers, then looks sharply across the hearth at Celeste.

"You told me she was trapped in the icehouse," Hollis snaps, his boots shifting on the stone.

"I barred the door and heard her inside!" Celeste shoots back, stepping backward toward the study archway. "I lit the shingles myself! If she got out, it wasn't through the front!"

"She's still inside the house," Sloane interrupts, dropping the Iridium chip back onto the lectern and snapping her leather briefcase latches open with two sharp metallic clicks. "And frankly, I don't care which closet she's hiding in, as long as she doesn't walk out with the wrong book. When that anonymous fax hit my Boston desk Tuesday night, I assumed my missing archivist Mara Abbott sent it before vanishing. I taped it to the back of the Blackthorne photo as a routine briefing exhibit—until your investigator flipped it over at Becky's Diner and went dead silent. I didn't even have to pay for the leverage. Here are the terms: I'm taking Arthur's body down the mountain in the back of Pike's plow before noon tomorrow so the state medical examiner certifies his time of death prior to five o'clock. My bondholders keep their thirty-eight million, and I wire a quiet two-hundred-thousand-dollar nuisance settlement to your Cayman account, Celeste, so nobody asks how Arthur's cardiac spray disappeared."

***

Sloane turns one crisp page inside her briefcase and pulls out a heavy silver lighter, clicking the cap open with her thumb.

"In exchange," Sloane continues, her voice dropping into dry granite, "you hand me the original **Caretaker's Ledger** tonight—the one Mara Abbott stole Tuesday night—so we burn it in this hearth before a single federal auditor looks at the 2020 escrow pages."

Silence hangs thick in the Great Hall, broken only by the pop of birch sap in the grate and the steady howl of wind against the high clerestory panes.

Celeste doesn't look at Hollis. She takes two quick steps across the flagstones toward Sloane, her pale face sharp and calculating in the firelight, the lavender oil on her fingers gleaming faintly as she clasps her hands in front of her cashmere sweater.

"I don't have the ledger," Celeste says quickly, her voice tight and eager. "Mara took it into the attic crawlspace Tuesday night. Hollis was the one who shot at her—Hollis was the one who burned the clinical manifests in this fireplace and poured the gasoline on the icehouse tonight. Give me half the settlement in writing right now, Sloane, and I'll hand you the study safe code and tell Pike where Hollis buried the *VaneCor* crates behind the carriage barn."

Hollis doesn't yell. He doesn't step forward to argue.

Through the iron scrollwork above him, I see the scarred stump of his missing right ring finger twitch once against the trigger guard of his twelve-gauge. His eyes lock onto the back of Celeste's blonde head with cold, dead stillness. Beside my cheek, the hot draft from the floor register smells of birch ash and damp wool.

"A nuisance settlement," Hollis repeats softly, almost under his breath. "Six years I drove cash envelopes up and down icy county roads for your husband, Celeste. Six years I kept my mouth shut while you watched him sign those kids off. Now you're selling me to State Street for a hundred grand and a clear name?"

"Hollis, put the barrel down," Sloane orders sharply, reaching inside her camel hair coat pocket. "If I miss my six-A.M. satellite check-in with Boston, my firm's compliance desk dispatches State Police to this gate."

Hollis swings the twin barrels upward—not at Sloane, not at Celeste.

He fires straight into the iron-and-glass **chandelier** hanging high overhead above the Great Hall hearth.

An ear-splitting **shotgun boom** shakes the rafters beneath my chest, rattling dust down from the floor register onto my coat collar and stinging my nose with cordite smoke. Thick leaded glass and twisted iron chains rain down across the flagstone floor in a blinding shower of sparks and shattered bulbs, plunging the room into half-darkness lit only by the dying birch coals.

Celeste screams and dives backward behind the oak reception lectern, knocking the sliced Guest Ledger and the blinking black Iridium chip onto the flagstones.

Before Sloane can draw whatever sits inside her coat pocket, Hollis vaults across the hearth rug in two heavy strides, slams his forearm across Sloane's throat, and hauls her backward toward the dark study archway—pressing the hot, smoking shotgun barrel tight beneath her jaw. Her leather briefcase hits the flagstones and slides across the hearth, spilling crisp bond schedules across the ash.

"Nobody's walking out that front door!" Hollis roars into the dark hall as glass crunches under his heavy boots. "Pike! Lock the iron gates and cut the SUV ignition right now!"
